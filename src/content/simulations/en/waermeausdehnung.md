## What is it about?

Almost all substances **expand when heated** and contract when cooled. In the particle model this is easy to see: warmer particles move more violently and need more space.

### Solids

A rod of length $L_0$ becomes longer by $\Delta L = \alpha \cdot L_0 \cdot \Delta T$. The **expansion coefficient** $\alpha$ depends on the material: aluminium expands about twice as much as steel, the special alloy Invar hardly at all. The changes are tiny – the simulation magnifies them. On long structures they add up: a 100 m steel bridge is several centimetres longer in summer, hence **expansion joints**.

A **bimetallic strip** consists of two bonded strips of different metals. When heated, one expands more and the strip bends – this switches irons, kettles and old thermostats.

### Liquids and gases

Liquids expand more than solids (thermometers). **Gases** expand much more, all by about the same amount: roughly $\frac{1}{273}$ of their volume at 0 °C per kelvin at constant pressure. Extending the line reaches zero volume at **−273 °C**, the **absolute zero**: $V = V_0 \cdot \frac{T}{T_0}$.

### The anomaly of water

Water becomes denser as it cools down to **4 °C**, but lighter below that. Ice is lighter still and floats. In autumn the surface water of a lake cools, sinks, until it is 4 °C everywhere; then colder water stays on top and the lake freezes **from the top**. At the bottom 4 °C water remains – fish survive.

## Exercises

**1. Bridge in summer.** A steel bridge is 100 m long at 20 °C ($\alpha = 12 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}}$). How much longer is it at 60 °C? [Load exercise](?mode=fest&mat=steel&L0=100&t=60&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$\Delta L = 12 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 100 \cdot 40\,\text{m} = 4.8\,\text{cm}$.

</details>

**2. Bimetal.** Aluminium on top, steel below, heated. Which way does it bend? [Load exercise](?mode=fest&mat=aluminium&t=80&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Aluminium expands more, so the strip bends **downwards** towards the steel side.

</details>

**3. Gas in a cylinder.** 1 L of air is heated at constant pressure from 20 °C to 80 °C. New volume? [Load exercise](?mode=gas&t=80&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$V = 1\,\text{L} \cdot \frac{353}{293} \approx 1.20\,\text{L}$.

</details>

**4. Fish in winter.** Why doesn't a deep lake freeze to the bottom?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Water at 4 °C is densest and stays at the bottom; colder water stays on top, freezes, and the floating ice insulates the lake.

</details>

## Ideas for teaching

- Ball-and-ring experiment, bimetal strip over a candle, balloon on a bottle in warm water.
- Why do power lines sag more in summer? Why do full bottles burst in the freezer?
