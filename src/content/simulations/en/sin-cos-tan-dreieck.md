## What is it about?

In a right triangle the side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse**. Seen from an acute angle, one leg is **adjacent** to the angle and the other is **opposite** it:

$$\sin \alpha = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \cos \alpha = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \qquad \tan \alpha = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

All right triangles with the same angle $\alpha$ are **similar**, so these ratios depend only on the angle. For $\beta$ the two legs swap roles, so $\sin \alpha = \cos \beta$.
