## What is it about?

Percent means **hundredths**: $1\,\% = \frac{1}{100}$, so $25\,\% = \frac{25}{100} = \frac14 = 0.25$. Every percentage problem has three quantities:

- the **base value** $G$ – the whole, it corresponds to $100\,\%$ (purple),
- the **rate** $p\,\%$ (orange),
- the **percentage value** $W$ – the part as a quantity, e.g. in euros (blue).

On the **percentage bar**, percentages are on top and values underneath. **Rule of three on the bar** (25 % of €80): split the bar into 100 equal parts, so $1\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €0.80$, and $25\,\% \;\hat{=}\; 25 \cdot €0.80 = €20$. To find the base value, go backwards: from the percentage value to $1\,\%$ and then to $100\,\%$.

$$
W = G \cdot \frac{p}{100}, \qquad p\,\% = \frac{W}{G}, \qquad G = W \cdot \frac{100}{p}
$$

With **20 % off** you pay $80\,\%$ of the old price ($G \cdot 0.8$); after a **20 % rise** it costs $120\,\%$ ($G \cdot 1.2$). German **VAT** is usually $19\,\%$, so the gross price is $119\,\%$ of the net price. **Pitfall:** if a bike costs €84 after a 20 % rise, then $€84 \;\hat{=}\; 120\,\%$, so it cost $€84 : 1.2 = €70$ before – not $€84 - 20\,\% = €67.20$.

## Try it

1. [Start](?G=80&p=25): press “Rule of three on the bar”. The bar splits into $100$ parts, then $25$ parts are counted. Watch the hundred square at the same time.
2. Drag the orange handle, tap the bar or tap squares in the hundred square: set $10\,\%$, $25\,\%$, $50\,\%$ and $75\,\%$. Which fractions belong to them? [Three quarters as a circle](?p=75&rep=pie)
3. [1 % of 250](?G=250&p=1): why is $1\,\%$ of $250$ equal to $2.5$? What is $10\,\%$ then?
4. [Find the base value](?find=G&G=240&p=15): start the animation – now it counts backwards, from $15\,\%$ via $1\,\%$ to $100\,\%$.
5. [Above 100 %](?G=80&p=150): the percentage value is larger than the base value. Where is the handle now?
6. Everyday life: [discount](?sit=rabatt&G=60&p=20), [price increase](?sit=erh&G=70&p=20), [receipt](?sit=mwst&G=50&p=19), [savings](?sit=zins&G=1200&p=2.5), [survey](?sit=klasse&find=p&G=48&p=25). Which part of the bar is the new price each time?
7. [Pitfall](?sit=erh&find=G&G=70&p=20): compare the two bars. Why does the red bar end before the purple line?

## Tasks

### Task 1: Percentage value

What is $25\,\%$ of €80? [Load task](?G=80&p=25&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$100\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €80$, $1\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €0.80$, $25\,\% \;\hat{=}\; 25 \cdot €0.80 = €20$. Shorter: $25\,\% = \frac14$, and a quarter of €80 is €20. [Show the solution](?G=80&p=25)

</details>

### Task 2: In the sale

A jumper costs €60. There is $20\,\%$ off. What does it cost now? [Load task](?sit=rabatt&G=60&p=20&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Discount: $€60 \cdot 0.2 = €12$, new price $€60 - €12 = €48$. Shorter: you pay $80\,\%$, so $€60 \cdot 0.8 = €48$. [Show the solution](?sit=rabatt&G=60&p=20)

</details>

### Task 3: The receipt

a) Headphones cost €50 before tax. What do they cost with $19\,\%$ VAT? [Load task](?sit=mwst&G=50&p=19&show=0&_hide=1)

b) A receipt shows €119 including $19\,\%$ VAT. What is the net price? [Load task](?sit=mwst&find=G&G=100&p=19&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

a) $19\,\%$ of €50 is €9.50, together €59.50; shorter $€50 \cdot 1.19 = €59.50$. [Show](?sit=mwst&G=50&p=19)

b) $€119 \;\hat{=}\; 119\,\%$, so $1\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €1$ and $100\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €100$. [Show](?sit=mwst&find=G&G=100&p=19)

</details>

### Task 4: The savings account

There is €1200 in a savings account at an interest rate of $2.5\,\%$ per year. How much interest is paid after one year, and what is the balance then? [Load task](?sit=zins&G=1200&p=2.5&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$1\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €12$, so $2.5\,\% \;\hat{=}\; 2.5 \cdot €12 = €30$ interest. Balance: $€1200 + €30 = €1230$. [Show the solution](?sit=zins&G=1200&p=2.5)

</details>

### Task 5: The pitfall

a) After a price rise of $20\,\%$ a bike costs €84. What did it cost before? [Load task](?sit=erh&find=G&G=70&p=20&show=0&_hide=1)

b) A price is first raised by $20\,\%$ and then lowered by $20\,\%$. Is it back to where it started?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

a) $€84 \;\hat{=}\; 120\,\%$, $1\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €0.70$, $100\,\% \;\hat{=}\; €70$. Check: $€70 \cdot 1.2 = €84$. Taking $20\,\%$ of €84 off (€67.20) is wrong, because the $20\,\%$ refer to the old price. [Show](?sit=erh&find=G&G=70&p=20)

b) No: $1.2 \cdot 0.8 = 0.96$. €100 becomes €120 and then €96 – in the end $4\,\%$ less than at the start, because the decrease refers to the larger base value of €120.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- With “Show the solution” switched off (`show=0`), the unknown appears as “?” – estimate on the bar first, then calculate and check with the animation. “New task” creates practice problems with friendly numbers.
- Bar, hundred square and circle show the same share: a bridge from $\frac34$ via $0.75$ to $75\,\%$.
- **Model:** amounts of money are rounded to the cent; values that are not exact appear with “≈”. Interest for one year only, without compound interest.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** mixing up base value and percentage value; confusing the discount or tax with the new price; subtracting the rate from the new value when working backwards ($€84 - 20\,\% \ne €70$); thinking that +20 % and −20 % cancel out; believing rates above 100 % are impossible; results such as $9.24$ children – a good reason to talk about sensible answers.
