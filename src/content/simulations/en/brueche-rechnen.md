## What is it about?

**Multiplying with an area model:** a square is one whole. The first fraction shades vertical strips (blue), the second horizontal strips (orange). Where both overlap (purple) is the **product**. For $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4}$ the square is cut into $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ equal cells, and $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ of them overlap:

$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \cdot 3}{3 \cdot 4} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

In general: **numerator times numerator, denominator times denominator**, so $\frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \cdot c}{b \cdot d}$.

- **“Of” means “times”:** $\frac{2}{3}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$ of a pizza is $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ of a pizza.
- **Multiplying does not always make bigger:** multiplying by a number less than $1$ makes the result smaller: $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4} < \frac{3}{4}$. Multiplying by a number greater than $1$ makes it bigger.
- **Cancel before multiplying:** you may cancel any numerator with any denominator, also **diagonally**: $\frac{4}{9} \cdot \frac{3}{8} = \frac{1 \cdot 1}{3 \cdot 2} = \frac{1}{6}$ ($4$ and $8$ by $4$, $3$ and $9$ by $3$). The numbers stay small.
- **Whole and mixed numbers** are written as fractions first: $3 = \frac{3}{1}$, $1\frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}$.

**Dividing means measuring:** $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3}$ asks: **how often does $\frac{2}{3}$ fit into $\frac{3}{4}$?** With a common denominator the pieces have the same size, and you only count pieces:

$$
\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{9}{12} \div \frac{8}{12} = 9 \div 8 = \frac{9}{8} = 1\tfrac{1}{8}
$$

So the strip $\frac{2}{3}$ fits in once completely, plus $\frac{1}{8}$ of it.

**The reciprocal rule:** $\frac{2}{3}$ fits into $1$ exactly $\frac{3}{2}$ times – this is the **reciprocal** of $\frac{2}{3}$. Into $\frac{3}{4}$ the strip fits $\frac{3}{4}$ times as often:

$$
\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{9}{8}
$$

In general: $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c}$ – you **multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor**. Dividing by a number less than $1$ makes the result bigger.

### Task 7: Bigger by dividing? (Puzzle)

Explain why $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{2}$ is greater than $\frac{3}{4}$. For which divisors is the result less than the dividend?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\frac{1}{2}$ fits into $\frac{3}{4}$ more than once, namely $\frac{3}{4} \cdot 2 = \frac{3}{2}$ times. Dividing by a number between $0$ and $1$ means multiplying by its reciprocal, which is greater than $1$. The result is only smaller if the divisor is greater than $1$. ## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** About grade 6, multiplying and dividing fractions: solid mental models “part of” (area model) and “sharing out/measuring” (division). For introducing the topic in class and for practice; dividing uses the idea of expanding to a common denominator from “Equivalent fractions and comparing”.
- **Model:** factors up to $3$ (so up to $3 \times 3$ wholes in the picture) and denominators up to $12$. Numerators are at least $1$, so division by $0$ cannot occur. The handles at the edge snap in steps of $\frac{1}{b}$ or $\frac{1}{d}$. With “Build up” the picture appears step by step: first one factor, then the other, then the overlap with numbered cells.
- **Division:** the simulation uses the idea of **measuring (quotition)**: “How often does … fit?”. If the divisor is a whole number, the idea of **sharing (partition)** is more natural: $\frac{3}{4} \div 3$ means “sharing $\frac{3}{4}$ among $3$ people”, so $\frac{1}{4}$ each. Both ideas lead to $\frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{3}$.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - “Multiplying makes bigger, dividing makes smaller.” – This is only true for factors or divisors greater than $1$.
  - Finding a common denominator before multiplying: not wrong, but unnecessary, and it makes the numbers big.
  - Taking the reciprocal of the wrong fraction: $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} \neq \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{3}$. Only the **divisor** is turned over.
  - Cancelling diagonally in additions: you must not cancel diagonally in $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{4}$ – that only works when multiplying.
