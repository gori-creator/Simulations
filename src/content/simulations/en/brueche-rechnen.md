## What is it about?

**Area model:** a square is one whole. The first fraction shades vertical strips (blue), the second horizontal strips (orange). The overlap (purple) is the **product**. For $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4}$ the square has $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ cells and $2 \cdot 3 = 6$ of them overlap:

$$
\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \cdot 3}{3 \cdot 4} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

“Of” means “times”: $\frac{2}{3}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$ is $\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{4}$. Multiplying by a number less than $1$ makes the result smaller. Before multiplying you may cancel any numerator with any denominator, also diagonally: $\frac{4}{9} \cdot \frac{3}{8} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{6}$.

**Division means measuring:** $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3}$ asks how often $\frac{2}{3}$ fits into $\frac{3}{4}$. With a common denominator the pieces have the same size: $\frac{9}{12} \div \frac{8}{12} = 9 \div 8 = \frac{9}{8}$. Since $\frac{2}{3}$ fits into $1$ exactly $\frac{3}{2}$ times (the **reciprocal**), you get $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{3}{2}$.

## Notes for teachers

- Grade 6 (Bavarian Gymnasium, learning area 1.4): “part of” for multiplication, “how often does it fit?” (measuring) for division. For whole-number divisors, sharing is the more natural idea: $\frac{3}{4} \div 3$ means sharing among $3$ people.
- Common misconceptions: “multiplying makes bigger”; taking the reciprocal of the wrong fraction; cancelling diagonally in additions.
