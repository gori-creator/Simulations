## What is it about?

An **angle** is formed by two rays, the **arms** $[SA$ and $[SB$, which start at the same point $S$. $S$ is called the **vertex**. Angles are named with Greek letters ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, …) or with three points: $\alpha = \angle ASB$. Here – as usual in Bavarian school books – $\angle ASB$ is measured **anticlockwise** from the arm $[SA$ to the arm $[SB$. The unit is the **degree** ($1^\circ$ is one 360th of a full turn).

| Type of angle | Size |
| --- | --- |
| zero angle | $\alpha = 0^\circ$ |
| acute angle | $0^\circ < \alpha < 90^\circ$ |
| right angle | $\alpha = 90^\circ$ |
| obtuse angle | $90^\circ < \alpha < 180^\circ$ |
| straight angle | $\alpha = 180^\circ$ |
| reflex angle | $180^\circ < \alpha < 360^\circ$ |
| full angle | $\alpha = 360^\circ$ |

**Measuring with a set square** in three steps:

1. Put the **zero mark** (middle of the long edge) exactly on the vertex $S$.
2. Put the **edge** exactly along one arm.
3. Read where the other arm crosses the scale – on the scale that **starts** at $0^\circ$ on the first arm. The set square has two scales: the outer one counts from the right, the inner one from the left.

**Reflex angles** are greater than $180^\circ$ and do not fit under the set square. Measure the **other angle** $\beta$ on the other side and calculate:

$$
\alpha = 360^\circ - \beta
$$

Example: $\beta = 130^\circ$, so $\alpha = 360^\circ - 130^\circ = 230^\circ$. With a **full-circle protractor** you can also read reflex angles directly.

**Estimating:** Compare with a right angle ($90^\circ$, “corner of a sheet of paper”) and a straight angle ($180^\circ$). Half a right angle is $45^\circ$.

## Try it

1. Drag the arm $[SB$ at its handle. When does the type of angle change? Watch the coloured bar.
2. Set [exactly 90°](?alpha=90&a1=35). How is the right angle marked?
3. Press “Random angle”: the set square now lies to one side and the size is hidden. Slide the set square with its zero mark onto $S$ and turn it at the round handles until the edge lies on an arm. Read off the angle!
4. For an [obtuse angle](?alpha=125&a1=200), place the set square on the **other** arm. Which scale do you have to read now?
5. For an [obtuse angle](?alpha=150&a1=0), slowly increase $\alpha$ beyond $180^\circ$. What do the instructions say? How do you measure now?
6. Measure the same [reflex angle](?alpha=230&a1=10) with the set square and with the [full circle](?alpha=230&a1=10&tool=voll).
7. Play the [estimation game](?mode=spiel): how many points can you get in ten tasks?

Name the type of each angle: $35^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $179^\circ$, $181^\circ$, $270^\circ$, $360^\circ$.

### Task 2: Reading the right scale

[Load task](?alpha=40&a1=180&show=0&_hide=1) Measure the angle $\alpha$. Leon reads $140^\circ$. What did he do wrong?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\alpha = 40^\circ$ – an acute angle, so the result must be less than $90^\circ$. Leon read the wrong scale: the two scales add up to $180^\circ$ ($40^\circ + 140^\circ = 180^\circ$). The right scale is the one that starts at $0^\circ$ on the arm along which the edge lies. [Show solution](?alpha=40&a1=180)

</details>

The other angle is $\beta = 65^\circ$, so $\alpha = 360^\circ - 65^\circ = 295^\circ$. [Show solution](?alpha=295&a1=60)

### Task 4: Angles on a clock

Through which angle does the minute hand turn in 10 minutes? And in 45 minutes?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

In 60 minutes it turns through $360^\circ$, so in one minute through $6^\circ$. 10 minutes: $60^\circ$ (acute). 45 minutes: $270^\circ$ (reflex). [Show 270°](?alpha=270&a1=180) – the hand turns clockwise from 12 o’clock ($[SB$) to 9 o’clock ($[SA$).

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** grade 5, learning area 2 “Geometric figures and relative positions” (LehrplanPLUS Gymnasium Bavaria): estimating, measuring and drawing angles up to $360^\circ$, types of angles. Well suited for working together at the whiteboard (placing the set square as a class) and for individual practice with the estimation game.
- **Handling:** The set square can be moved in the middle and turned at the round handles or the corners. It snaps to the vertex and to the arms. With “Show the size of the angle” off, students really have to read the size; the highlight then only shows which scale to use.
- **Model:** Set square and grid match: the edge is 16 cm long, one square is 5 mm. Angles are rounded to whole degrees. “Random angle” hides the size and puts the set square to one side.
- **Estimation game:** Tasks alternate between estimating and drawing an angle; the size and the direction of the first arm are random. Points depend on the difference from the correct value: up to $3^\circ$ three points, up to $10^\circ$ two, up to $20^\circ$ one, otherwise none. After checking, the set square is ready for measuring.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - Reading the wrong scale (the result and the correct value add up to $180^\circ$) – a plausibility check with the type of angle helps.
  - Longer arms mean a larger angle. The arms are rays; their drawn length does not matter.
  - The zero mark is placed at the corner of the set square instead of on the vertex.
  - For reflex angles, the other angle is given as the result.
- **Notation:** The angle $\angle ASB$ is measured anticlockwise from $[SA$ to $[SB$, so $\angle ASB + \angle BSA = 360^\circ$.
