## What is it about?

The **mean** is the sum of all values divided by their number: $\bar{x} = \frac{x_1 + \ldots + x_n}{n}$. It is the **balance point** of the data on the number line.

The **median** is the middle value of the **ordered list**. With an even number of values it is the mean of the two middle values.

The median splits the ordered list into a lower and an upper half (with an odd count the median itself belongs to neither). The **lower quartile** $Q_1$ is the median of the lower half, the **upper quartile** $Q_3$ the median of the upper half. Some books and programs define quartiles slightly differently.

The **box plot** shows minimum, $Q_1$, median, $Q_3$ and maximum. The box goes from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$ and holds about the middle half of the data. The **range** is maximum − minimum, the **interquartile range** is $Q_3 - Q_1$. Values more than $1.5$ times the interquartile range outside the box are often marked as **outliers**.

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- Students could collect their own data (way to school, sleep, long jump) and type it into the “Values” field; comparison mode puts two surveys side by side.
- The quartiles are computed as medians of the two halves; if your textbook uses another convention, a short note may help.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** reading the median from the unordered list; taking only one of the two middle values; reading a long whisker as “more values”; treating mean and median as the same.
