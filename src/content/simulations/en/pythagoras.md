## What is it about?

In a **right triangle** the sides next to the right angle are the **legs** $a$ and $b$; the side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse** $c$.

> **Pythagorean theorem:** $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$

So $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ and $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$. **Converse:** if $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$, the triangle is right-angled.

### Puzzle proof

Four copies of the triangle are placed in a square with side $a + b$. In the first arrangement the free area is $c^2$, in the second it is $a^2 + b^2$. The big square and the triangles are the same, so the free areas are equal.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Hypotenuse

Legs $a = 1.5\,\text{cm}$, $b = 2\,\text{cm}$. Find $c$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$c = \sqrt{2.25 + 4} = \sqrt{6.25} = 2.5\,\text{cm}$. [Show in the simulation](?a=1.5&b=2)

</details>

### Exercise 2: Ladder

A $5\,\text{m}$ ladder stands $1.4\,\text{m}$ from a wall. How high does it reach?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$h = \sqrt{25 - 1.96} = \sqrt{23.04} = 4.8\,\text{m}$.

</details>

### Exercise 3: Right-angled or not?

Check the triangles with sides $7, 24, 25$ and $5, 6, 8$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$: right-angled. $25 + 36 = 61 \neq 64$: not right-angled.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- **Use:** Grade 8/9. Counting squares works best with integer triples like 3-4-5.
- **Misconceptions:** applying the theorem to non-right triangles; choosing the wrong side as $c$; $\sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = a + b$.
