## What is it about?

An electron confined to a small region – a long molecule or a tiny semiconductor crystal – behaves like a **wave**. In the simplest model it sits in a **potential well** of width $L$ with infinitely high walls.

### Standing waves and energy levels

As on a fixed string, only **standing waves** fit: $\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}$. With $p = \frac{h}{\lambda}$ the allowed energies are

$$
E_n = \frac{h^2}{8 m L^2} \cdot n^2
$$

Energy is **quantised**; the levels spread out as $n^2$, and the narrower the well, the larger the gaps. Even in the **ground state** the particle is never at rest.

### Probability

The **wave function** $\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ gives, via $|\psi|^2$, the probability density; the area under $|\psi|^2$ in a region is the probability of finding the particle there. For $n = 2$ the particle is **never** exactly in the middle (a node); for large $n$ the distribution becomes almost uniform.

### Transitions and light

A jump to a lower level emits a **photon** with $\frac{h c}{\lambda} = E_{n_1} - E_{n_2}$ – explaining the colours of some dyes and quantum dots. Nucleons in a nucleus ($L \approx 10\,\text{fm}$) have MeV energies – gamma rays.

## Exercises

**1. Energy levels.** Electron in a 1 nm well: $E_1$ and $E_3$? [Load exercise](?Lnm=1&n=3&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$E_1 \approx 0.376\,\text{eV}$, $E_3 = 9 E_1 \approx 3.38\,\text{eV}$.

</details>

**2. Narrower well.** What happens to the energies if the well is half as wide?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

They become **four times** as large – smaller quantum dots glow bluer.

</details>

**3. Which colour?** Jump from $n = 2$ to $n = 1$ in a 0.65 nm well. [Load exercise](?Lnm=0.65&n=2&n2=1&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$\lambda = \frac{8 m L^2 c}{3 h} \approx 465\,\text{nm}$ – blue light.

</details>

**4. Probability.** Ground state, middle fifth ($0.4L$ to $0.6L$)? [Load exercise](?n=1&xa=0.4&xb=0.6&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

About 39 %; classically 20 %.

</details>
