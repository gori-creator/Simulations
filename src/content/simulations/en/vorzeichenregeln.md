## What is it about?

The number line helps with adding and subtracting integers. But what does $(-3) \cdot (-4)$ mean? The answer is $+12$, and there are good reasons for it. The simulation shows three of them.

### The sign rules

| Calculation | Sign of the result | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| plus · plus | plus | $3 \cdot 4 = 12$ |
| plus · minus | minus | $3 \cdot (-4) = -12$ |
| minus · plus | minus | $(-3) \cdot 4 = -12$ |
| minus · minus | plus | $(-3) \cdot (-4) = 12$ |

In short: **same signs give plus, different signs give minus.** You work out the size of the result as usual: $|-3| \cdot |-4| = 12$. If one factor is $0$, the result is $0$.

### 1. Jumps and the opposite number on the number line

$3 \cdot (-4)$ means three times $-4$, so three jumps of length $4$ to the left. You land on $-12$.

A negative first factor means "the opposite of that". For $(-3) \cdot (-4)$ you first jump as for $3 \cdot (-4)$, down to $-12$. Then you reflect at zero and get the opposite number $12$.

### 2. Continuing the pattern (permanence principle)

If you write calculations one below the other, a pattern appears:

$$3 \cdot (-2) = -6,\quad 2 \cdot (-2) = -4,\quad 1 \cdot (-2) = -2,\quad 0 \cdot (-2) = 0$$

Each time the first factor goes down by $1$, the result goes up by $2$. For the pattern to hold below zero as well, $(-1) \cdot (-2)$ must be $2$, then $(-2) \cdot (-2) = 4$, and so on. The rules for negative numbers are chosen so that the familiar laws of arithmetic still hold.

### 3. The debt model

An account holds credit notes and IOUs. The first factor says how many cards are **added** (plus) or **taken away** (minus). The second factor is the value of one card, and an IOU counts as negative.

- Three IOUs of €5 are added: $3 \cdot (-5) = -15$. The account gets worse.
- Three IOUs of €5 are cancelled: $(-3) \cdot (-5) = 15$. The account gets **better** by €15.

### Dividing

Dividing undoes multiplying: $-12 : 3$ is the number that gives exactly $-12$ when multiplied by $3$. That is $-4$, because $(-4) \cdot 3 = -12$. So the **same** sign rules apply to division:

$$-12 : 3 = -4,\qquad -12 : (-3) = 4,\qquad 12 : (-3) = -4$$

You cannot divide by $0$, because no number times $0$ gives anything other than $0$.

### Several factors

With several factors, count the minus signs. If there is an even number of them, the result is positive. If there is an odd number, it is negative. Example: $(-1) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-3) = -6$, because there are three minus signs.

For **powers** of a negative base the exponent decides: $(-2)^4 = 16$, $(-2)^5 = -32$. Careful: $-2^4$ without brackets means $-(2^4) = -16$.

### The times table

The times table from −6 to 6 splits into **four quarters**: top left and bottom right only positive numbers, top right and bottom left only negative ones.

### Everyday: temperature over the day

The temperature falls by 3 °C per hour. How warm was it 4 hours **ago**? Change per hour $-3$, time $-4$: $(-3) \cdot (-4) = +12$ – it was 12 °C warmer.

### Order of operations with negative numbers

With signs too: multiply and divide first, then add and subtract. Then combine **operation sign and number sign**: $+\,(-3)$ becomes $-\,3$, and $-\,(-3)$ becomes $+\,3$. Example:

$6 + (-2) \cdot 3 - (-8) : 2 = 6 + (-6) - (-4) = 6 - 6 + 4 = 4$

### Removing brackets

If there is a factor in front of a bracket, every number inside is multiplied by it. A **minus in front of the bracket** is the factor $-1$: every sign inside flips.

$-(3 - 5 + 1) = -3 + 5 - 1 \qquad -3 \cdot (2 + 4 - 6) = -6 - 12 + 18$

## Exercises

**1. Finding the sign.** Calculate $(-6) \cdot 4$ and $(-6) \cdot (-4)$. [Load exercise](?mode=strahl&a=-6&b=4&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$(-6) \cdot 4 = -24$ (different signs) and $(-6) \cdot (-4) = 24$ (same signs).

</details>

**2. Pattern.** Continue: $2 \cdot (-3) = -6$, $1 \cdot (-3) = -3$, $0 \cdot (-3) = 0$, $(-1) \cdot (-3) = \;?$ [Load exercise](?mode=muster&a=-1&b=-3&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

The result goes up by $3$ in every row, so $(-1) \cdot (-3) = 3$.

</details>

**3. Balance.** The balance is −€20. Four IOUs of €5 are cancelled. What is the balance afterwards? [Load exercise](?mode=schulden&st=-20&k=-4&w=-5&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$-20 + (-4) \cdot (-5) = -20 + 20 = 0$. The account is balanced at €0.

</details>

**4. Dividing.** Calculate $-30 : (-6)$ and $30 : (-6)$. [Load exercise](?mode=teilen&dd=-30&dv=-6&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$-30 : (-6) = 5$, because $5 \cdot (-6) = -30$. And $30 : (-6) = -5$.

</details>

**5. Several factors.** Is $(-2) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-2)$ positive or negative?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

There are four minus signs, which is an even number. The result is positive: $16$.

</details>

**6. Temperature.** The temperature has been rising by 2 °C per hour since morning. It is now 8 °C. How warm was it 5 hours ago? [Load task](?mode=alltag&ts=8&rt=2&hh=-5&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Change: $2 \cdot (-5) = -10$. Five hours ago it was $8 - 10 = -2$ °C.

</details>

**Removing a bracket.** Remove the bracket and work it out: $-2 \cdot (4 - 7 + 1)$. [Load task](?mode=klammer&kf=-2&k1=4&k2=-7&k3=1&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$-8 + 14 - 2 = 4$. Check: $-2 \cdot (-2) = 4$.

</details>

## Ideas for teaching

- Start with the pattern – let the class continue $3 \cdot (-2), 2 \cdot (-2), \dots$ past zero themselves.
- Common mistake: “minus times minus is plus” does not apply to adding: $(-3) + (-4) = -7$.
