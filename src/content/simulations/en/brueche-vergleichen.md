## What is it about?

**Expanding** a fraction means multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number. In the picture every piece is cut into equal parts – more, but smaller pieces. The amount stays the same: $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{12}$.

**Reducing** is the reverse: divide numerator and denominator by the same number, i.e. join equal groups of pieces. This only works if **both** are divisible by that number. A fraction is **fully reduced** when numerator and denominator have no common divisor except $1$: $\frac{12}{18} = \frac{2}{3}$ (divide by the gcd $6$).

**Comparing:** with equal denominators, compare the numerators. Otherwise use a **common denominator** – the smallest one is the lcm of the denominators:

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{12} < \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}
$$

With **equal numerators**, the fraction with the larger denominator has smaller pieces and is smaller: $\frac{4}{9} = \frac{12}{27} < \frac{12}{22} = \frac{6}{11}$.

### Task 2: Compare

Which is larger, $\frac{7}{12}$ or $\frac{5}{8}$?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Common denominator $24$: $\frac{14}{24} < \frac{15}{24}$, so $\frac{7}{12} < \frac{5}{8}$. [Show](?z1=7&n1=12&z2=5&n2=8)

</details>

### Task 3: Order

Order $\frac{3}{4}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{5}{8}$, $\frac{7}{12}$ from smallest to largest.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

In $24$ths: $18, 16, 15, 14$. So $\frac{7}{12} < \frac{5}{8} < \frac{2}{3} < \frac{3}{4}$.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- Three views: comparing (bars or circles with a shared number line), expanding and reducing (steps with arcs, group brackets, fraction wall) and a game for ordering fractions.
- With `show=0` students tap the comparison sign themselves.
- Pieces are numbered only once they have equal size, so learners are not invited to compare numerators of different-sized pieces.
- **Misconceptions:** “larger denominator, larger fraction”; comparing only numerators; “$\frac{3}{4} = \frac{4}{5}$ because one piece is missing each time”.
