## What is it about?

**Expanding** a fraction means multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number. In the picture every piece is cut again into equal parts – there are more, but smaller pieces. The **amount stays the same**:

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \cdot 3}{4 \cdot 3} = \frac{9}{12}
$$

**Reducing** is the reverse: divide numerator and denominator by the same number. In the picture, groups of equally many pieces are joined into one. This only works if numerator **and** denominator are divisible by that number. A fraction is **fully reduced** when numerator and denominator have no common divisor except $1$. The quickest way is to reduce by the gcd:

$$
\frac{12}{18} = \frac{12 : 6}{18 : 6} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

Fractions that come from each other by expanding or reducing are **equivalent**: they describe the same amount and lie at the **same place** on the number line. In the **fraction wall** they end exactly above each other.

**Comparing fractions:**

- **Same denominator:** the pieces have the same size, so you compare the numerators: $\frac{9}{12} < \frac{10}{12}$.
- **Different denominators:** give the fractions a **common denominator**. The smallest one is the lcm of the denominators, the **lowest common denominator**:

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{9}{12} < \frac{10}{12} = \frac{5}{6}
$$

- **Same numerator:** both have equally many pieces. The fraction with the **larger denominator** has the smaller pieces and is therefore **smaller**: $\frac{4}{9} = \frac{12}{27} < \frac{12}{22} = \frac{6}{11}$.

On the number line, the smaller fraction lies further to the left.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Expand and reduce

a) Expand $\frac{2}{5}$ by $4$. b) Reduce $\frac{24}{36}$ fully. [Load task](?mode=kuerzen&z=24&n=36&f=2&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

a) $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{8}{20}$. b) $\gcd(24, 36) = 12$, so $\frac{24}{36} = \frac{2}{3}$. Step by step works too: $\frac{24}{36} = \frac{12}{18} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$ (reduced by $2$, $2$ and $3$). [Show in one step](?mode=kuerzen&z=24&n=36&f=12)

</details>

### Task 2: Why not by 4?

Explain why $\frac{6}{8}$ cannot be reduced by $4$. [Load task](?mode=kuerzen&z=6&n=8&f=4)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$8$ is divisible by $4$, but $6$ is not. If you join groups of $4$ eighths, the shading ends in the middle of a group: the numerator would be $6 : 4 = 1.5$ – not a whole number. Reducing by $2$ works: $\frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$.

</details>

### Task 3: Compare

Fill in $<$, $=$ or $>$: a) $\frac{2}{3} \;\square\; \frac{3}{5}$ b) $\frac{7}{12} \;\square\; \frac{5}{8}$ c) $\frac{6}{9} \;\square\; \frac{8}{12}$ [Load task a](?z1=2&n1=3&z2=3&n2=5&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

- a) Common denominator $15$: $\frac{10}{15} > \frac{9}{15}$, so $\frac{2}{3} > \frac{3}{5}$.
- b) Common denominator $24$: $\frac{14}{24} < \frac{15}{24}$, so $\frac{7}{12} < \frac{5}{8}$. [Show](?z1=7&n1=12&z2=5&n2=8)
- c) Both reduce to $\frac{2}{3}$, so $\frac{6}{9} = \frac{8}{12}$. [Show](?z1=6&n1=9&z2=8&n2=12)

</details>

### Task 4: Equal numerators

Which fraction is larger, $\frac{3}{7}$ or $\frac{6}{11}$? Compare using equal numerators.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\frac{3}{7} = \frac{6}{14}$. Now both fractions have $6$ pieces; fourteenths are smaller than elevenths. So $\frac{6}{14} < \frac{6}{11}$, i.e. $\frac{3}{7} < \frac{6}{11}$. [Show](?z1=3&n1=7&z2=6&n2=11&weg=zaehler)

</details>

### Task 5: Pizza

Lea eats $\frac{3}{8}$ of a pizza, Tim eats $\frac{2}{5}$ of a pizza of the same size. Who eats more, and by how much?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Common denominator $40$: $\frac{3}{8} = \frac{15}{40}$ and $\frac{2}{5} = \frac{16}{40}$. Tim eats more, namely $\frac{1}{40}$ of the pizza. [Show as circles](?z1=3&n1=8&z2=2&n2=5&form=kreis)

</details>

### Task 6: Ordering

Order by size: $\frac{3}{4}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{5}{8}$, $\frac{7}{12}$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

With the common denominator $24$: $\frac{18}{24}$, $\frac{16}{24}$, $\frac{15}{24}$, $\frac{14}{24}$. So $\frac{7}{12} < \frac{5}{8} < \frac{2}{3} < \frac{3}{4}$.

</details>

### Task 7: A fraction in between (puzzle)

Find a fraction that lies between $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

With a common denominator: $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{3}{6}$ – there is no sixth in between. Expanding further to $\frac{4}{12}$ and $\frac{6}{12}$ gives $\frac{5}{12}$ in between. Between two different fractions there is always another fraction.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** grade 6 (in Bavaria: LehrplanPLUS Gymnasium, learning area 1.1 “Fractions of a whole and fractions as numbers”): expanding, reducing, comparing and fractions on the number line. Common denominators are needed again for adding fractions (learning area 1.3). The simulation “Representing fractions” works as an introduction, this one for deepening and practice.
- **Three views:** *Compare* (two fractions as bars or circles with a shared number line), *Expand and reduce* (steps with arcs “·3” or “:2”, group brackets and fraction wall) and the game *Order fractions* (three levels, with hint bars).
- **Guess instead of reading off:** with `show=0` (switch “Show the comparison sign right away” off) three buttons appear instead of the sign; students tap $<$, $=$ or $>$ themselves and get immediate feedback. Together with “Hide results (for exercises)” this works well for tasks shared by link or QR code.
- **Deliberate design:** pieces are numbered only once they have equal size (after finding a common denominator). This way learners are not invited to compare numerators of different-sized pieces.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - “Larger denominator, larger fraction” ($\frac{1}{8} > \frac{1}{4}$) – the view with equal numerators shows that the pieces get smaller.
  - Comparing only the numerators: for $\frac{3}{4} < \frac{5}{6}$ this happens to work, for $\frac{5}{8} > \frac{3}{5}$ it does not.
  - “$\frac{3}{4}$ and $\frac{4}{5}$ are equal because one piece is missing each time.” The missing pieces have different sizes; the difference is shown as a hatched area.
  - “Expanding makes the fraction larger.” The shaded part and the point on the number line do not change when expanding.
  - Reducing by crossing out equal digits: $\frac{16}{64} = \frac{1}{4}$ is only right by chance, $\frac{12}{24} = \frac{1}{4}$ is wrong.
- **Note:** comparing via equal numerators is a useful addition to the common denominator, especially when the lcm of the denominators is large.
