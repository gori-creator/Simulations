## What is it about?

Every magnet has a **north pole** (usually red in schools) and a **south pole** (usually green). **Unlike poles attract, like poles repel.** The force quickly gets weaker with distance, and both magnets feel forces of equal size in opposite directions.

Only **iron, nickel and cobalt** (and materials containing them, such as steel) are attracted. A piece of iron is attracted by **either** pole, because it briefly becomes a magnet itself. Aluminium, copper and gold are not attracted.

The **magnetic field** can be made visible with **iron filings** or **compass needles**. Field lines leave at the **north pole** and enter at the **south pole**; where they are dense, the field is strong.

**Elementary magnets:** iron can be pictured as many tiny magnets. When they are disordered they cancel out; stroking with a magnet lines them up. Hammering or strong heating (above $768\,^\circ\text{C}$ for iron) mixes them up again. A broken magnet gives two complete magnets.

**The Earth is a magnet.** There is a **magnetic south pole** near the **geographic North Pole**, so the north pole of a compass needle points north. In Germany the field lines dip into the ground by about $65°$ to $68°$ (**inclination**).

## Tasks

### Task 1

A magnet hanging from a thread turns until one end points north. Is that end a north or a south pole?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

A north pole – it is attracted by the Earth's magnetic south pole near the geographic North Pole.

</details>

### Task 2

A bar magnet breaks in the middle. Do you get a single north pole and a single south pole?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

No. Each piece is again a complete magnet with a north and a south pole.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- Let pupils sort everyday objects first and compare with the game – the 1 cent coin is often a surprise.
- Draw the field line pattern by moving the compass step by step and copying the needle directions.
- Discuss what the elementary-magnet model explains well and where it stops.
