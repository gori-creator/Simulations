## What is it about?

In a **reflection in a line** $s$, a point $P$ and its image $P'$ lie on a perpendicular to $s$ at the same distance from $s$: $s$ bisects $PP'$ at right angles. In a **reflection in a point** $Z$, $Z$ is the midpoint of every segment $PP'$ – the same as a **half turn** ($180^\circ$) about $Z$.

Both keep lengths and angles. A reflection in a line **reverses the orientation** ($A \to B \to C$ anticlockwise becomes clockwise); a reflection in a point keeps it. Fixed points: all points on $s$, or only $Z$. Fixed lines: $s$ and every perpendicular to $s$, or every line through $Z$.

### Task 2: Letters

Which of the letters H, T, E, N, F have line symmetry, which have point symmetry? [Check](?mode=symmetrie&fig=N)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

H: two lines and point symmetry. T, E: one line each. N: point symmetry only. F: no symmetry.

</details>

### Task 3: The parallelogram

Are the diagonals of a parallelogram lines of symmetry? [Load figure](?mode=symmetrie&fig=parallelogramm&rot=15)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

No (unless it is a rhombus). But a parallelogram has point symmetry about the intersection of its diagonals.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- Grade 7: properties of reflections, constructing images with a set square, recognising symmetry. All points snap to the grid (one square $= 0.5\,\text{cm}$).
- Switch off “Show the image” for construction tasks. Wrong attempts in “find the symmetry” show the non-matching image in red.
- Common misconceptions: reflecting “horizontally” instead of perpendicular to a slanted axis; “a diagonal of a parallelogram is a line of symmetry”; “a reflection in a point reverses the orientation”.
