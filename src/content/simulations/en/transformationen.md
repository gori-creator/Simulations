## What is it about?

From a **basic function** $f$ we get $g(x) = a \cdot f\big(b\,(x - c)\big) + d$:

- $a$: vertical stretch by factor $a$ (reflection in the $x$-axis if $a < 0$),
- $b$: horizontal stretch by factor $\frac{1}{b}$ (reflection in the $y$-axis if $b < 0$),
- $c$: shift by $c$ to the right, $d$: shift by $d$ up.

A point $(x \mid y)$ of $f$ becomes $\left(\frac{x}{b} + c \mid a y + d\right)$. Careful: $f(x - 2)$ moves **right**, and $f(2x)$ is **compressed** horizontally.

How far is $g(x) = (2x - 4)^2$ shifted compared with $f(x) = (2x)^2$?

<details>

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- The same rules for different basic functions show that the effect of the parameters does not depend on the type of function.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** $f(x - 2)$ moves right, $f(2x)$ is compressed, forgetting to factor out $b$ in $f(2x - 4)$.
