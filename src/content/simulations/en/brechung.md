## What is it about?

At the boundary between two transparent materials light is partly reflected and partly refracted. All angles are measured from the **normal**. Snell’s law:

$$
n_1 \sin\alpha = n_2 \sin\beta
$$

Entering an optically denser material, light bends towards the normal. Going into a less dense material, there is a **critical angle** $\alpha_G$ with $\sin\alpha_G = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$; beyond it, light is totally reflected (water → air $48.8^\circ$, glass → air $41.1^\circ$, diamond → air $24.4^\circ$).

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Angle of refraction

Light goes from air into water at $40^\circ$. Find $\beta$. [Load exercise](?alpha=40&_hide=1)

<details>

$\sin\beta = \sin 40^\circ / 1.33 \approx 0.483$, so $\beta \approx 28.9^\circ$.

## Notes for teachers

- The brightness of the rays follows the Fresnel equations for unpolarised light.
- **Misconception:** angles are measured from the surface instead of the normal.
