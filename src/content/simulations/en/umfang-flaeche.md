## What is it about?

A rectangle has a **length** $a$ and a **width** $b$.

**Area $A$ – how much surface does it cover?** We count **unit squares**: a square with side $1\,\text{cm}$ has an area of $1\,\text{cm}^2$. A $5\,\text{cm} \times 3\,\text{cm}$ rectangle holds $3$ rows of $5$ squares:

$$
A = a \cdot b = 5\,\text{cm} \cdot 3\,\text{cm} = 15\,\text{cm}^2
$$

**Perimeter $P$ – how long is the edge all the way round?** It is a length, measured in $\text{cm}$:

$$
P = 2 \cdot a + 2 \cdot b = 2 \cdot (a + b) = 2 \cdot (5\,\text{cm} + 3\,\text{cm}) = 16\,\text{cm}
$$

Unrolled like a string, the edge gives two equal pieces of length $a + b$. For a **square**, $A = a \cdot a$ and $P = 4 \cdot a$.

Rectangles with the same perimeter can have very different areas – the square has the largest. With the same area, long thin strips have the largest perimeter. An **L-shape** can be split into two rectangles ($A = A_1 + A_2$) or completed to a big rectangle ($A = a \cdot b - c \cdot d$); its perimeter equals that of the big rectangle.

## Try it

1. Drag the corner and watch area and perimeter change.
2. Press **Tile and count**: the first row is laid square by square, then copied upwards – that is why $A = a \cdot b$. [7 cm × 4 cm](?a=7&b=4)
3. Press **Let the ant walk** and count the centimetres. Why are the two pieces of string equally long?
4. Compare the [square 4 cm × 4 cm](?a=4&b=4) with the [strip 10 cm × 1 cm](?a=10&b=1&faden=1).
5. [Same perimeter](?mode=gleichU&u=20&w=1) and [same area](?mode=gleichA&fa=24&w=1): which rectangle is the "best"?
6. Split the [L-shape](?mode=lform&a=6&b=5&c=3&d=2) in different ways and push its inner edges outwards.

### Task 1

Find the area and perimeter of an $8\,\text{cm} \times 3\,\text{cm}$ rectangle. [Load task](?a=8&b=3&show=0&_hide=1)

$A = 8\,\text{cm} \cdot 3\,\text{cm} = 24\,\text{cm}^2$, $P = 2 \cdot (8\,\text{cm} + 3\,\text{cm}) = 22\,\text{cm}$. [Show](?a=8&b=3)

### Task 2

A square has a perimeter of $20\,\text{cm}$. Find its side length and its area.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$4 \cdot a = 20\,\text{cm}$, so $a = 5\,\text{cm}$ and $A = 25\,\text{cm}^2$. [Show](?a=5&b=5)

</details>

### Task 3

You have $24\,\text{m}$ of fencing for a rectangular flower bed with whole-metre sides. Which bed has the largest area? (Use $1\,\text{cm}$ for $1\,\text{m}$.) [Load task](?mode=gleichU&u=24&w=1&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$a + b = 12\,\text{m}$; the square $6\,\text{m} \times 6\,\text{m}$ gives the largest area, $36\,\text{m}^2$. [Show](?mode=gleichU&u=24&w=6)

</details>

### Task 4

An $8\,\text{cm} \times 6\,\text{cm}$ rectangle has a $3\,\text{cm} \times 4\,\text{cm}$ piece cut from its top right corner. Find the area in two ways, and the perimeter. [Load task](?mode=lform&a=8&b=6&c=3&d=4&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$8 \cdot 2 + 5 \cdot 4 = 36$ and $8 \cdot 6 - 3 \cdot 4 = 36$, so $A = 36\,\text{cm}^2$. $P = 2 \cdot (8\,\text{cm} + 6\,\text{cm}) = 28\,\text{cm}$. [Show](?mode=lform&a=8&b=6&c=3&d=4&zerl=erg&kanten=1)

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- **Use:** Year 5 (Bavarian Gymnasium), measures and units – area. Introduce with tiling and the ant, practise with task links (`show=0&_hide=1`), extend with the comparison modes and the L-shape.
- **Model:** whole-centimetre sides; the paper shows whole and half centimetres, as in German squared exercise books (one $1\,\text{cm}^2$ = four small squares).
- **Misconceptions:** comparing perimeter and area directly; "larger perimeter means larger area"; swapping the formulas; forgetting the inner edges of an L-shape.
