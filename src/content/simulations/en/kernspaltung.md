## What is it about?

Nuclei consist of protons and neutrons (**nucleons**). The strong force binds them so tightly that a nucleus is **lighter** than its parts – the **mass defect**, which by $E = mc^2$ equals the **binding energy** $B$.

### The binding-energy curve

$B/A$ rises steeply for light nuclei, peaks at **iron** (about 8.8 MeV) and falls slowly for heavy nuclei. Hence:

- **Fission:** a heavy nucleus such as U-235 splits into two medium nuclei that are more tightly bound – about 200 MeV per fission is released.
- **Fusion:** very light nuclei fuse (D + T → He-4); per nucleon even more energy is released. This powers the Sun.

Burning coal releases only a few eV per molecule – millions of times less.

### Chain reaction

A U-235 nucleus capturing a neutron splits and releases **2–3 neutrons**. The **multiplication factor** $k$ decides: $k < 1$ subcritical (dies out), $k = 1$ critical (power plant), $k > 1$ supercritical (exponential growth). **Control rods** absorb neutrons to regulate $k$.

## Exercises

**1. Fission energy.** U-235: about 7.6 MeV per nucleon, fragments about 8.5 MeV. Estimate the energy released. [Load exercise](?mode=bindung&A=235&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

About $235 \cdot 0.9\,\text{MeV} \approx 210\,\text{MeV}$ (measured about 200 MeV).

</details>

**2. Why not iron?** Can energy be gained from iron-56? [Load exercise](?mode=bindung&A=56&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

No – iron is at the maximum of the curve.

</details>

**3. Critical reactor.** 2.5 neutrons per fission, 30 % escape. What share must the control rods absorb for $k = 1$? [Load exercise](?mode=kette&stab=40&leck=30&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$2.5 \cdot 0.7 \cdot (1 - x) = 1$, so about **43 %**.

</details>

**4. Avalanche.** With $k = 2$, starting from one neutron, how many after 10 generations?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$2^{10} = 1024$ – within about $10^{-7}\,\text{s}$ in a bomb.

</details>
