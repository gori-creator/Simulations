## What is it about?

The **unit circle** is the circle around the origin with radius 1. A point $P$ moves on it; the angle $\alpha$ is measured from the positive x-axis **anticlockwise**.

The coordinates of $P$ are exactly the cosine and sine of the angle:

$$
P\,(\cos\alpha,\ \sin\alpha)
$$

This defines sine and cosine not only for acute angles in right triangles but for **all angles**.

- The **tangent** is $\tan\alpha = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha}$ – on the unit circle it is the length of the segment on the tangent line $x = 1$.
- By Pythagoras, $\sin^2\alpha + \cos^2\alpha = 1$ always holds.

In **radians**, an angle is measured by the length of its arc on the unit circle. A full turn has length $2\pi$, so $360^\circ$ corresponds to $2\pi$.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Values without a calculator

Find $\sin 120^\circ$ and $\cos 120^\circ$ using symmetry.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$P$ is the mirror image of the point for $60^\circ$ in the y-axis: $\sin 120^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\cos 120^\circ = -\frac{1}{2}$. [Check](?angle=120)

</details>

### Exercise 2: Solve an equation

For which angles between 0° and 360° is $\sin\alpha = 0.5$?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\alpha_1 = 30^\circ$ and, since $\sin(180^\circ - \alpha) = \sin\alpha$, $\alpha_2 = 150^\circ$.

</details>
