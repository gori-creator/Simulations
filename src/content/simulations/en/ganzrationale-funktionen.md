## What is it about?

A polynomial with enough real zeros can be written in **factored form** $f(x) = a (x - x_1)^{m_1} (x - x_2)^{m_2} \cdots$ with **multiplicities** $m_i$, **leading coefficient** $a$ and **degree** $n = m_1 + m_2 + \ldots$

- Odd multiplicity: the sign changes (simple zero: crosses; triple zero: terrace point).
- Even multiplicity: no sign change (touches the axis).
- **End behaviour:** for $x \to +\infty$, $f(x)$ has the sign of $a$; for even $n$ both ends go the same way, for odd $n$ opposite ways.

## Try it

1. Drag the zeros and the point on the right (leading coefficient).
2. Compare a [double zero](?z=2&x1=-2&m1=2&x2=2&m2=1&a=0.5) with a [triple zero](?z=2&x1=1&m1=3&x2=-3&m2=1&a=0.3).
3. Move two simple zeros onto each other. What happens?

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- With properties hidden, students can play “graph → term” in pairs.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** the sign inside the bracket (the zero of $(x + 2)$ is $-2$), expecting a crossing at every zero, ignoring the degree for the end behaviour.
