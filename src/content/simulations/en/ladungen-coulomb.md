## What is it about?

All matter consists of atoms with positive nuclei and negative **electrons**. Usually the charges balance – the body is **electrically neutral**.

### Charging by friction

Rubbing two different materials moves **electrons** from one to the other. The one giving electrons becomes **positive**, the other **negative**. The total stays zero – charge is not created but **separated**. The **triboelectric series** tells which material gives electrons: glass on silk becomes positive, plastic on wool becomes negative.

- **Like** charges repel.
- **Unlike** charges attract.

### Electroscope

Touching an **electroscope** with a charged rod spreads charge onto the rod and leaves; both leaves carry like charge and **repel**. The deflection shows that there is charge, not its sign. **Earthing** removes it.

### Coulomb's law

$$
F = k \cdot \frac{Q_1 \cdot Q_2}{r^2} \qquad k \approx 8.99 \cdot 10^9\,\frac{\text{N m}^2}{\text{C}^2}
$$

Double the charge – double the force. Double the distance – only a **quarter** of the force.

### Induction: charging without touching

A negative rod brought near a neutral metal sphere pushes the mobile electrons to the far side; the near side becomes positive while the sphere as a whole is still neutral. This is **induction**. Earth the sphere briefly and the repelled electrons flow away; remove the rod and the sphere stays **positively** charged – without ever being touched.

### Field lines

Every charge is surrounded by an **electric field**. Field lines show the direction a small positive test charge would be pulled: away from plus, towards minus. The denser the lines, the stronger the field; they never cross.

### In the atom: electric force versus gravity

Electron and proton attract electrically and by gravity. Both fall off as $\frac{1}{r^2}$, but the electric force is about $2.3 \cdot 10^{39}$ times larger – so it governs atoms and materials.

## Exercises

**1. Which charge?** A glass rod is rubbed with silk. Which is positive? [Load exercise](?mode=reiben&ma=glass&mb=silk&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Glass gives electrons to the silk and becomes **positive**; rod and cloth attract.

</details>

**2. Electroscope.** A negative rod touches a neutral electroscope. What does it show, and how can you find the sign? [Load exercise](?mode=skop&ma=pvc&mb=wool&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

The leaves spread; bring a rod of **known** charge near – the leaves spread further for like charges and close for unlike charges.

</details>

**3. Coulomb force.** +2 µC and −1 µC at 20 cm. Force? At 10 cm? [Load exercise](?mode=coulomb&q1=2&q2=-1&r=20&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

About 0.45 N (attraction); four times as much, about 1.8 N, at 10 cm.

</details>

**4. In the atom.** Electron and proton at $5.3 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{m}$. Force?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

About $8.2 \cdot 10^{-8}\,\text{N}$ – some $10^{39}$ times the gravitational force between them.

</details>

**5. Charged by induction.** A positive rod is brought near a neutral metal sphere, the sphere is earthed briefly, then the rod is removed. What charge does the sphere have? [Load task](?mode=influenz&sgn=plus&d=8&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Negative: the positive rod attracts electrons, and on earthing extra electrons flow in from the earth and stay after the rod is removed.

</details>

## Ideas for teaching

- Balloon rubbed on hair sticks to the wall; a charged rod bends a thin water jet.
- Why do you get a shock from the door handle after walking on carpet?
- Common mistake: field lines are not paths of charges – they show the direction of the force on a positive test charge.
