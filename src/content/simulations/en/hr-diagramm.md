## What is it about?

Plotting stars by **surface temperature** (decreasing to the right) and **luminosity** $L$ gives the **Hertzsprung–Russell diagram**. Most stars lie on the **main sequence**, where they fuse hydrogen into helium. Top right are **red giants** and **supergiants**, bottom left **white dwarfs**.

**Stefan–Boltzmann law:**

$$
\frac{L}{L_\odot} = \left(\frac{R}{R_\odot}\right)^{2} \cdot \left(\frac{T}{T_\odot}\right)^{4}, \qquad T_\odot \approx 5772\,\text{K}.
$$

Double the temperature at the same radius gives 16 times the luminosity.

**Wien's law:** $\lambda_{\max} = 2.898\,\text{mm·K} / T$, about $502\,\text{nm}$ for the Sun. Spectral classes run O, B, A, F, G, K, M from hot to cool.

**Life of a star:** mass decides. On the main sequence $t \approx 10\,\text{billion years} \cdot M/L$. Sun-like stars end as white dwarfs; stars above about $8\,M_\odot$ explode as supernovae and leave a neutron star or a black hole.

**Parallax:** $d = 1/p$ ($d$ in parsec, $p$ in arcseconds), $1\,\text{pc} \approx 3.26$ light years.

## Tasks

### Task 1

A star has the Sun's radius but twice its temperature. What is its luminosity?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$1^2 \cdot 2^4 = 16\,L_\odot$.

</details>

### Task 2

Proxima Centauri has a parallax of $0.768''$. How far away is it?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$d = 1/0.768\,\text{pc} \approx 1.30\,\text{pc} \approx 4.25$ light years.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- Start with Orion: why is Betelgeuse reddish and Rigel bluish? Find both in the diagram.
- Common pitfalls: "red stars are hotter", "big stars live longer", the temperature axis decreasing to the right.
- Star data are rounded; the evolutionary path is schematic.
