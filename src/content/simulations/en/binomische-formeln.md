## What is it about?

The three **binomial formulas** are shortcuts for expanding brackets:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a + b)^2 &= a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \\
(a - b)^2 &= a^2 - 2ab + b^2 \\
(a + b)(a - b) &= a^2 - b^2
\end{aligned}
$$

As **areas**: a square with side $a + b$ consists of $a^2$, $b^2$ and **two** rectangles $a \cdot b$. Forgetting these rectangles leads to the common mistake $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Mental arithmetic

Calculate $53^2$, $49^2$ and $21 \cdot 19$ using a binomial formula.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$53^2 = 2500 + 300 + 9 = 2809$, $49^2 = 2500 - 100 + 1 = 2401$, $21 \cdot 19 = 400 - 1 = 399$.

</details>

### Exercise 2: Expanding

Expand $(x + 4)^2$, $(2x - 3)^2$ and $(5 + y)(5 - y)$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$x^2 + 8x + 16$, $\;4x^2 - 12x + 9$, $\;25 - y^2$.

</details>

### Exercise 3: Factorising

Write as a product: $x^2 + 10x + 25$ and $49 - y^2$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$(x + 5)^2$ and $(7 + y)(7 - y)$.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- **Use:** Grade 7/8 as an introduction and as a justification of the middle term $2ab$.
- **Misconceptions:** $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$; $(2x)^2 = 2x^2$; the $+b^2$ in the second formula feels wrong – the animation shows that $b^2$ is subtracted twice.
- **Limits of the model:** areas need positive lengths; the formulas hold for all numbers.
