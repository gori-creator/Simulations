## What is it about?

Without friction the total energy stays constant:

$$
m g h_0 = m g h + \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{2 g (h_0 - h)}
$$

The mass cancels out: heavy and light cars are equally fast. With friction, part of the energy is converted into internal energy. To get round a loop of radius $r$ without lifting off, the car needs $v^2 \ge g r$ at the top, i.e. a starting height of at least $2.5\,r$.

## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Speed in the valley

The car starts in the valley from $15\,\text{m}$. How fast is it at the bottom? [Load exercise](?track=tal&h0=15&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$v = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 15} \approx 17.2\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \approx 62\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}$.

</details>

### Exercise 2: Minimum height for the loop

Show that without friction the car must start at least at $2.5\,r = 15\,\text{m}$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

At the top ($h = 2r$): $v^2 \ge g r$. Energy: $m g h_0 = 2 m g r + \tfrac12 m v^2 \ge 2.5\, m g r$, so $h_0 \ge 2.5\,r$. [Show 16 m](?track=looping&h0=16)

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- **Use:** Conservation of energy (grade 9/10); the loop links to circular motion.
- **Misconception:** "With friction energy is lost" – it is converted, not destroyed.
