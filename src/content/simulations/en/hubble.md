## What is it about?

In 1929 **Edwin Hubble** compared the distances of galaxies with their spectra. Almost all spectral lines were shifted towards red, the more so the farther away the galaxy was. Read as a velocity: **the farther away, the faster a galaxy recedes.**

$$
v = H_0 \cdot d \qquad \text{(Hubble’s law)}
$$

Today $H_0 \approx 70\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{s}\cdot\text{Mpc}}$ (1 Mpc ≈ 3.26 million light years). A galaxy 100 Mpc away recedes at about $7000\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{s}}$, $z = \tfrac{\lambda - \lambda_0}{\lambda_0} \approx \tfrac{v}{c} \approx 0.023$.

Every distance grows by the same factor, so from every galaxy all others seem to recede – there is no centre. Strictly, space itself stretches, and light stretches with it: $1 + z = a_\text{today}/a_\text{then}$. Running the expansion backwards gives the Hubble time $1/H_0 \approx 14.0$ billion years; the standard model ($\Omega_m = 0.3$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.7$) gives an age of about 13.5 billion years. The light released 380,000 years after the Big Bang ($T \approx 3000$ K) reaches us today as microwave background radiation at 2.7 K.

## Tasks

### Task 1

How fast does a galaxy 200 Mpc away recede ($H_0 = 70$)?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$v = 14\,000\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{s}}$ – about 652 million light years away.

</details>

### Task 2

Hα (656.3 nm) is observed at 721.9 nm. Find $z$, $v$ and $d$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$z \approx 0.10$, $v \approx 30\,000\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{s}}$, $d \approx 428\,\text{Mpc}$.

</details>

### Task 3

A type Ia supernova ($M = -19.3$) appears with $m = 15.7$. How far away is it?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$m - M = 35$, so $d = 10^{8}\,\text{pc} = 100\,\text{Mpc}$.

</details>
