## What is it about?

Every triangle has four kinds of **special lines**, three of each – and each time all three meet at **one** point:

- **Perpendicular bisectors** → circumcentre $U$, equally far from the vertices ($UA = UB = UC = r$).
- **Angle bisectors** → incentre $I$, equally far ($\rho$) from all sides.
- **Medians** (vertex to midpoint of opposite side) → centroid $S$, which divides each median in the ratio $2 : 1$.
- **Altitudes** → orthocentre $H$.

$I$ and $S$ always lie inside. $U$ and $H$ lie inside an acute triangle and outside an obtuse one; in a right triangle $U$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse and $H$ is the right-angled vertex. **Euler line:** $U$, $S$ and $H$ are collinear with $HS = 2 \cdot SU$.

## Tasks

### Task 1: A right triangle

$A(2|2)$, $B(10|2)$, $C(2|8)$ in cm. Where are $U$ and $H$, and what is $r$? [Load task](?ax=2&ay=2&bx=10&by=2&cx=2&cy=8&pt=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$U(6|5)$ is the midpoint of the hypotenuse $BC = 10\,\text{cm}$, so $r = 5\,\text{cm}$. $H = A(2|2)$, because the legs are altitudes. [Show](?ax=2&ay=2&bx=10&by=2&cx=2&cy=8)

### Task 2: Balancing

Where does a cardboard triangle balance on a pin? How far from $A$ is that point if the median from $A$ is $6\,\text{cm}$ long?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

At the centroid $S$, $\frac{2}{3} \cdot 6\,\text{cm} = 4\,\text{cm}$ from $A$. [Show](?m=sh)

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- With the point switched off, students can form the conjecture “the three lines meet at one point” themselves.
- Groups investigate one kind of line each for acute, right and obtuse triangles and collect the results in a table.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** confusing perpendicular bisectors with medians, or altitudes with angle bisectors; believing the circumcentre is always inside; forgetting to extend sides for altitudes in obtuse triangles.
