## What is it about?

**Directly proportional:** double $x$ gives double $y$; all quotients $\frac{y}{x}$ are equal and $y = kx$ (a ray through the origin). Apples at $2.50\,€$ per kg: $3\,\text{kg}$ cost $7.50\,€$.

**Inversely proportional:** double $x$ gives half of $y$; all products $x \cdot y$ are equal and $y = \frac{k}{x}$ (a branch of a hyperbola, rectangles of equal area). One person needs $12$ h, three people need $4$ h.

Careful: a taxi with a base fare is “the more, the more”, but **not** proportional.

## Try it

1. [Apples](?kind=dir&kd=2.5&x=3): drag $P$ and look at the column $y : x$.
2. [Garden bed](?kind=ind&ki=12&x=3): compare the rectangles.
3. [Taxi](?kind=lin&kt=2&b=3&x=3): why don’t $3\,\text{km}$ cost three times as much as $1\,\text{km}$?
4. [Double x](?kind=ind&ki=12&x=2): press “Double x” several times – what happens to $y$?
5. [Identify the relation](?mode=qz&qs=1): practise with everyday tables. Careful with the [candle](?mode=qz&qs=2): “the more – the less” is not enough for inverse proportionality.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Working together

Two people need $12$ h for a bed. How long do $4$ people need? [Load task](?kind=ind&ki=24&x=4&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

One person: $24$ h; four people: $24 : 4 = 6$ h. [Show](?kind=ind&ki=24&x=4)

</details>

### Task 2: The taxi

Base fare $3\,€$, $2\,€$ per km: do $3\,\text{km}$ cost $15\,€$?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

No: $3 + 3 \cdot 2 = 9\,€$. The base fare is paid only once. [Show](?kind=lin&kt=2&b=3&x=3)

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- Let students collect everyday examples and sort them into the three types first.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** equating “the more, the more” with proportionality, multiplying instead of dividing for inverse proportion, drawing the inverse graph as a line.
