## What is it about?

**White light** is a mixture of all colours. A **prism** shows it: glass refracts blue and violet light slightly more than red, so the light fans out and a **spectrum** appears on the screen – the same order as in a rainbow. This splitting is called **dispersion**. Beyond red lies invisible **infrared**, beyond violet invisible **ultraviolet**.

### Colour filters

A **colour filter** lets part of the light through (**transmission**) and swallows the rest (**absorption**). A red filter passes only red, a yellow filter red and green.

### Additive mixing – light plus light

With the three primaries **red, green and blue (RGB)**: red + green = **yellow**, green + blue = **cyan**, red + blue = **magenta**, all three = **white**. Screens work this way.

### Subtractive mixing – filter behind filter

Filters, ink and paint **remove** light. The primaries are **cyan, magenta and yellow (CMY)**: cyan absorbs red, magenta absorbs green, yellow absorbs blue. Cyan + yellow = **green**, all three = **black**. Printers add black (K) – hence **CMYK**.

## Exercises

**1. Mixing light.** A red and a blue spotlight shine on the same spot of a white wall. Which colour appears? [Load exercise](?mode=add&r=100&g=0&b=100&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Magenta** – red and blue add up; green is missing.

</details>

**2. Filters in a row.** White light passes a yellow filter and then a cyan filter. What comes out? [Load exercise](?mode=sub&c=100&m=0&y=100&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Yellow absorbs blue, leaving red and green; cyan then absorbs red. **Green** remains.

</details>

**3. Red letters in green light.** A red poster on a white background is lit only with green light. What does it look like?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

The background looks **green**, the red letters look **black**.

</details>

**4. Screen.** Which dots of a screen light up for white, yellow and black? [Load exercise](?mode=pixel&r=100&g=100&b=0&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

White: all three. Yellow: red and green. Black: none.

</details>

## Ideas for teaching

- Torches and coloured foils show additive mixing on a wall; stacked foils show subtractive mixing.
- Look at a screen through a magnifier and compare with the simulation.
- A phone camera can "see" the infrared of a remote control.
