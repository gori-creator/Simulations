## What is it about?

A **power** is a short way to write a product of equal factors: $10^6 = 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 1\,000\,000$. The lower number is the **base**, the small raised number the **exponent** – it tells you how many times the base is used as a factor. Likewise $2^5 = 32$ and $3^4 = 81$.

$10^n$ is a $1$ followed by $n$ zeros: $10^3 = 1\,000$, $10^6$ is one million, $10^9$ one billion. By definition $10^0 = 1$ – each step down divides by $10$.

Every step in the exponent makes a length 10 times as large: $1\,\text{mm} = 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$ (grain of sand), $1\,\text{m}$ (child), $100\,\text{m}$ (football pitch), $1\,\text{km} = 10^3\,\text{m}$, $10^7\,\text{m}$ (the Earth is about $12\,742\,\text{km}$ across). So $1\,\text{km} = 10^3\,\text{m}$ and $1\,\text{m} = 10^3\,\text{mm}$.

**Scientific notation** writes numbers as $a \cdot 10^n$ with $1 \le a < 10$: $35\,000 = 3.5 \cdot 10^4$, $1\,270\,000 = 1.27 \cdot 10^6$. *Outlook:* small numbers get negative exponents, e.g. $0.0042 = 4.2 \cdot 10^{-3}$.

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- The journey works well as a whole-class starter; the cube models show that “exponent + 1” means “times the base”. With results hidden, pupils can estimate sizes from the grid first.
- Objects are drawn to scale; only the airliner and the space station are enlarged symbols.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** $10^3$ calculated as $10 \cdot 3$; mixing up $2^5$ and $5^2$; thinking $10^0 = 0$; writing $35 \cdot 10^3$ instead of $3.5 \cdot 10^4$.
