## What is it about?

A **power** is a short way to write a product of equal factors: $10^6 = 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 1\,000\,000$. The lower number is the **base**, the small raised number the **exponent** – it tells you how many times the base is used as a factor. Likewise $2^5 = 32$ and $3^4 = 81$.

$10^n$ is a $1$ followed by $n$ zeros: $10^3 = 1\,000$, $10^6$ is one million, $10^9$ one billion. By definition $10^0 = 1$ – each step down divides by $10$.

Every step in the exponent makes a length 10 times as large: $1\,\text{mm} = 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$ (grain of sand), $1\,\text{m}$ (child), $100\,\text{m}$ (football pitch), $1\,\text{km} = 10^3\,\text{m}$, $10^7\,\text{m}$ (the Earth is about $12\,742\,\text{km}$ across). So $1\,\text{km} = 10^3\,\text{m}$ and $1\,\text{m} = 10^3\,\text{mm}$.

**Scientific notation** writes numbers as $a \cdot 10^n$ with $1 \le a < 10$: $35\,000 = 3.5 \cdot 10^4$, $1\,270\,000 = 1.27 \cdot 10^6$. *Outlook:* for numbers smaller than $1$ the point moves to the right and the exponent becomes negative: $10^{-1} = 0.1$, $10^{-3} = 0.001$ and $0.0042 = 4.2 \cdot 10^{-3}$. In year 5 this is an outlook; in year 6 you will learn it in more detail.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Working out powers

Work out $10^4$, $2^5$ and $3^4$. Write down the product first each time. [Load task](?mode=pow&b=2&n=5&show=0&_hide=1)

### Task 2: Million and billion

Write one million and one billion as powers of ten. How many zeros do the numbers have? How many millions make a billion? [Load task](?mode=pow&b=10&n=6&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$1\,000\,000 = 10^6$ (6 zeros), $1\,000\,000\,000 = 10^9$ (9 zeros). $10^9 = 10^6 \cdot 10^3$, so $1\,000$ millions make a billion. [Show in the simulation](?mode=pow&b=10&n=9)

</details>

### Task 3: Estimating sizes

Set $1$ square $= 10\,\text{m}$. Use the squares to estimate how tall the house is. How many children standing on each other would be as tall? [Load task](?e=1&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The house is about $10\,\text{m} = 10^1\,\text{m}$ tall. The child is about $1.4\,\text{m}$ tall; $10 \div 1.4 \approx 7$, so about seven children standing on each other would be as tall as the house. [Show in the simulation](?e=1)

</details>

### Task 4: Converting units

Write as a power of ten in metres: $1\,\text{km}$, $1\,\text{cm}$ and $1\,\text{mm}$. How many millimetres are $1\,\text{km}$?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$1\,\text{km} = 1\,000\,\text{m} = 10^3\,\text{m}$, $1\,\text{cm} = 0.01\,\text{m} = 10^{-2}\,\text{m}$, $1\,\text{mm} = 0.001\,\text{m} = 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$ (negative exponents as an outlook). $1\,\text{km} = 10^3 \cdot 10^3\,\text{mm} = 10^6\,\text{mm} = 1\,000\,000\,\text{mm}$. [Show in the simulation](?e=3)

</details>

### Task 5: Scientific notation

Write $35\,000$ and $1\,270\,000$ in the form $a \cdot 10^n$ with $1 \le a < 10$. [Load task](?mode=sci&z=1270000&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$35\,000 = 3.5 \cdot 10^4$ (point moves 4 places to the left), $1\,270\,000 = 1.27 \cdot 10^6$ (6 places to the left). Check: $1.27 \cdot 1\,000\,000 = 1\,270\,000$. [Show in the simulation](?mode=sci&z=1270000)

</details>

### Task 6: Outlook – very small numbers

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** grade 5 (powers and powers of ten with large numbers), grade 6 for revision, scientific notation and negative exponents such as $10^{-3} = 0.001$ (depending on the curriculum also later).
- The journey works well as a whole-class starter; the cube models show that “exponent + 1” means “times the base”. With results hidden, pupils can estimate sizes from the grid first.
- Objects are drawn to scale; only the airliner and the space station are enlarged symbols.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** $10^3$ calculated as $10 \cdot 3$; mixing up $2^5$ and $5^2$; thinking $10^0 = 0$; writing $35 \cdot 10^3$ instead of $3.5 \cdot 10^4$.
