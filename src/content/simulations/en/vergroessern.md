## What is it about?

Scaling a figure with the **scale factor** $k$ produces a **similar** figure: lengths are multiplied by $k$, **areas** by $k^2$ and **volumes** by $k^3$. A cube with twice the edge length consists of $2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 8$ unit cubes. This also holds for reductions ($0 < k < 1$): with $k = 0.5$ the area is a quarter, the volume an eighth.

2. [k = 3](?k=3) and [k = 4](?k=4): how many cubes now?
3. [k = 0.5](?k=0.5): how many small cubes fit into the original?
4. [Cuboid](?obj=q&k=2): does the rule hold for a cuboid as well?

## Tasks

### Task 1: Three times as large

A $1\,\text{cm}$ cube is scaled with $k = 3$. Find edge, surface area and volume. [Load task](?k=3&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Edge $3\,\text{cm}$, surface $6 \cdot 9 = 54\,\text{cm}^2$, volume $27\,\text{cm}^3$. [Show](?k=3)

</details>

### Task 2: Model railway

A model is built at scale $1 : 87$. By which factors are the real wagon’s surface area and volume larger?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Area $87^2 = 7{,}569$ times, volume $87^3 = 658{,}503$ times.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- Build $k = 2$ and $k = 3$ with snap cubes and compare.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** “double the side – double the area”; expecting $k^2 > k$ for $k < 1$.
