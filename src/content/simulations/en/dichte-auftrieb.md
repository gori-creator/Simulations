## What is it about?

**Density** tells you how much mass there is in one cubic centimetre of a material: $\rho = \frac{m}{V}$ (in $\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$; $1\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} = 1000\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$).

A liquid pushes an immersed body upwards with the **buoyant force**. By **Archimedes’ principle** it equals the weight of the displaced liquid:

$$
F_B = \rho_{liquid} \cdot V_{\text{displaced}} \cdot g
$$

A $100\text{-cm}^3$ body fully under water feels $F_B \approx 0.98\,\text{N}$, whatever it is made of. Comparing densities decides what happens: a body **floats** if $\rho_{body} < \rho_{liquid}$ (the share below the surface is $\rho_{body}/\rho_{liquid}$, e.g. $60\,\%$ for wood of $0.6\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$, $91.7\,\%$ for ice), it **hovers** if the densities are equal and it **sinks** if $\rho_{body} > \rho_{liquid}$.

A **steel ship** floats because its mean density (mass divided by outer volume) is smaller than that of water; a **submarine** floods or empties its ballast tanks. Warm air is less dense than cold air ($\rho = \frac{p \cdot M}{R \cdot T}$) – that is how a **hot-air balloon** rises.

### Task 1: Tip of the iceberg

What share of an iceberg ($0.917\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$) is below the surface of fresh water? [Load task](?mat=ice&_hide=1)

### Task 2: Spring balance in water

An aluminium cube ($200\,\text{cm}^3$, $2.7\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$) is fully lowered into water. What does the spring balance read? [Load task](?mode=arch&mat=alu&V=200&d=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$F_G = 0.54\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\tfrac{\text{N}}{\text{kg}} \approx 5.30\,\text{N}$; $200\,\text{g}$ of water overflow, so $F_B \approx 1.96\,\text{N}$ and the reading is $3.34\,\text{N}$. [Lower it](?mode=arch&mat=alu&V=200)

</details>

### Task 3: Mystery sample

Find the density of sample A. What is it made of? [Load task](?mode=rho&probe=A&pos=cyl&_hide=1)

<details>

### Task 4: Ship in a river and at sea

The $300\,\text{g}$ steel ship ($20\,\text{cm} \times 10\,\text{cm}$) carries $600\,\text{g}$. How deep does it sit in fresh water and in sea water? [Load task](?obj=ship&load=600&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

It displaces $900\,\text{g}$: $900\,\text{cm}^3 / 200\,\text{cm}^2 = 4.5\,\text{cm}$; at sea $900 / (1.025 \cdot 200) \approx 4.39\,\text{cm}$. [Show at sea](?obj=ship&load=600&liq=salt)

</details>

### Task 5: Hot-air balloon

A $2500\,\text{m}^3$ balloon weighs $315\,\text{kg}$. Outside air $1.225\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$, inside ($90\,°\text{C}$) $0.972\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$. What is the largest payload? [Load task](?mode=ball&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$(1.225 - 0.972) \cdot 2500 \approx 632\,\text{kg}$, minus $315\,\text{kg}$: about $317\,\text{kg}$. [Try it](?mode=ball&ml=320)

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** density as a property of materials, pressure and buoyancy (grades 7–9); the balloon links to the gas law.
- **Like a lab:** the mystery samples work well in pairs. Without the wire (`push=0`) wood gives a density that is far too large – a nice error to discover.
- **Model:** $g = 9.81\,\tfrac{\text{N}}{\text{kg}}$, fresh water $1.00\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$; no tilting; waves exaggerated for clarity; the balloon’s outside temperature is the same at all heights.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - “Heavy things sink.” – a ship weighs thousands of tonnes; what matters is (mean) density.
  - “Buoyancy depends on the material of the body.” – only on the displaced volume and the liquid.
  - “Deeper down the buoyancy is larger.” – once fully immersed, $F_B$ stays the same.
