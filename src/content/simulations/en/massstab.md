## What is it about?

A map shows reality **reduced**. The **scale** tells you by how much. A hiking map says, for example, **1 : 25,000** (read “one to twenty-five thousand”). This means:

$$
1\,\text{cm on the map} \;\hat{=}\; 25\,000\,\text{cm} = 250\,\text{m in reality}
$$

The number after the colon is the **scale number**. You can calculate in both directions:

- **Map → reality:** map length **times** the scale number. Example: $4\,\text{cm} \cdot 25\,000 = 100\,000\,\text{cm} = 1\,000\,\text{m} = 1\,\text{km}$.
- **Reality → map:** convert the real length to cm, then **divide** by the scale number. Example: $2.5\,\text{km} = 250\,000\,\text{cm}$ and $250\,000\,\text{cm} : 25\,000 = 10\,\text{cm}$.
- **Finding the scale:** put both lengths in the same unit and divide. Example: $6\,\text{cm}$ on the map are $3\,\text{km} = 300\,000\,\text{cm}$; $300\,000 : 6 = 50\,000$, so $1 : 50\,000$.

A map with a **small scale number** (e.g. $1 : 5\,000$) shows a small area with many details – you can see every house. A map with a **large scale number** (e.g. $1 : 100\,000$) shows a large area with few details. The **scale bar** at the bottom left of the map is divided into pieces of $1\,\text{cm}$ each and shows directly how far one centimetre is in reality.

Very small things are **enlarged**. Then the larger number comes first: **10 : 1** means $10\,\text{cm}$ in the picture are $1\,\text{cm}$ in reality. A $7\,\text{mm}$ ladybird is $7\,\text{cm}$ long in the picture; back to reality: $7\,\text{cm} : 10 = 0.7\,\text{cm} = 7\,\text{mm}$.

*Note:* The centimetres in the simulation are the centimetres of the drawn ruler – on screen they may be a little larger or smaller than real centimetres, depending on the device. Lengths are always measured to $1\,\text{mm}$, just like with a school ruler.

## Tasks

### Task 1: From the map to reality

On a map at the scale $1 : 50\,000$, two places are $3\,\text{cm}$ apart. How far apart are they in reality? [Load task](?mode=calc&cq=real&cm=3&cs=50000&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$3\,\text{cm} \cdot 50\,000 = 150\,000\,\text{cm} = 1\,500\,\text{m} = 1.5\,\text{km}$. [Show in the simulation](?mode=calc&cq=real&cm=3&cs=50000)

</details>

### Task 2: Town plan

On the town plan ($1 : 5\,000$), measure the straight line from the school to the town hall and convert it. [Load task](?s=5000&ax=8240&ay=6680&bx=7760&by=6820&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The line is $10\,\text{cm}$ long on the map: $10\,\text{cm} \cdot 5\,000 = 50\,000\,\text{cm} = 500\,\text{m}$. [Show in the simulation](?s=5000&ax=8240&ay=6680&bx=7760&by=6820)

</details>

### Task 3: From reality to the map

The bathing spot is $2.5\,\text{km}$ from the church as the crow flies. How long is this line on a map at the scale $1 : 25\,000$? [Load task](?mode=calc&cq=map&rw=2500&cs=25000&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$2.5\,\text{km} = 250\,000\,\text{cm}$; $250\,000\,\text{cm} : 25\,000 = 10\,\text{cm}$. [Measure on the map](?s=25000&ax=8000&ay=7000&bx=9500&by=5000)

</details>

### Task 4: Finding the scale

On a map, two places are $6\,\text{cm}$ apart; in reality they are $3\,\text{km}$ apart. What is the scale of the map? [Load task](?mode=calc&cq=scale&cm=6&rw=3000&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$3\,\text{km} = 300\,000\,\text{cm}$; $300\,000\,\text{cm} : 6\,\text{cm} = 50\,000$. The map has the scale $1 : 50\,000$. On the map: church to lookout tower. [Show in the simulation](?s=50000&ax=8000&ay=7000&bx=6200&by=9400)

</details>

### Task 5: Enlargement

An ant is $5\,\text{mm}$ long. How long is it in a picture at the scale $10 : 1$? And a grain of sand with a diameter of $0.4\,\text{mm}$ at the scale $50 : 1$? Work it out yourself first – the **Enlarge** view shows the result straight away.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Ant: $5\,\text{mm} \cdot 10 = 50\,\text{mm} = 5\,\text{cm}$. Grain of sand: $0.4\,\text{mm} \cdot 50 = 20\,\text{mm} = 2\,\text{cm}$. [Show the ant](?mode=zoom&o=ant&z=10) · [Show the grain of sand](?mode=zoom&o=sand&z=50)

</details>

### Task 6: Straight line or route?

From the church to the bathing spot, the straight line on the map ($1 : 25\,000$) is $10\,\text{cm}$. Measured piece by piece along the road, the route is $10.7\,\text{cm}$ in total. How long are both in reality, and how much longer is the route? [Load task](?s=25000&r=1&ax=8000&ay=7000&bx=9500&by=5000&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Straight line: $10\,\text{cm} \cdot 25\,000 = 250\,000\,\text{cm} = 2.5\,\text{km}$. Route: $10.7\,\text{cm} \cdot 25\,000 = 267\,500\,\text{cm} = 2\,675\,\text{m} \approx 2.7\,\text{km}$. The route is $175\,\text{m}$ longer than the straight line. [Show in the simulation](?s=25000&r=1&ax=8000&ay=7000&bx=9500&by=5000)

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** Grade 5, measures (length, scale) in the Bavarian LehrplanPLUS, Gymnasium – please check against your curriculum. Use the map as a shared introduction on the projector and the “New measuring task” with the result hidden for practice in pairs.
- **Link to a real map:** Afterwards, have the children measure their way to school on a hiking map of the area and convert it.
- **Model:** The landscape is invented. As on real maps, houses and map symbols are drawn somewhat larger than they would be to scale. The map length is measured to the nearest $1\,\text{mm}$; the calculation uses this measured value.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - The unit is forgotten: $4 \cdot 25\,000 = 100\,000$ are **centimetres**, not metres.
  - The number of zeros is mixed up when converting cm to km – correct is $1\,\text{km} = 100\,000\,\text{cm}$.
  - “Large scale” is confused with “large scale number”: $1 : 5\,000$ is the larger scale; it shows more details.
  - When finding the scale, lengths in different units are divided ($3\,\text{km} : 6\,\text{cm}$).
  - With enlargements, students multiply instead of dividing ($7\,\text{cm} : 10$ is correct, not $7\,\text{cm} \cdot 10$).
