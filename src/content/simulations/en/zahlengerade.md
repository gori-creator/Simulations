## What is it about?

The **integers** are $\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ On the **number line** the negative numbers lie to the left of $0$, the positive numbers to the right. The further right a number lies, the greater it is: $-5 < -2 < 0 < 3$.

- The **opposite** of a number is its mirror image at $0$: the opposite of $-3$ is $3$, the opposite of $3$ is $-3$.
- The **absolute value** is the distance from $0$: $\lvert -3 \rvert = 3$ and $\lvert 3 \rvert = 3$.

**Arrow model:** a number is drawn as an arrow – positive numbers point to the right, negative numbers to the left. When adding, you place the arrow at the first number.

**Walking model:** a frog stands on the first number and faces the positive numbers.

1. The **operation sign** tells it where to face: with $+$ it keeps facing right, with $-$ it **turns around**.
2. The **sign of the second number** tells it how to hop: positive number **forwards**, negative number **backwards** – as many hops as the absolute value says.

This gives the rules:

$$
a - b = a + (-b) \qquad \text{and} \qquad a + (-b) = a - b
$$

So subtracting means **adding the opposite**. For example $2 - (-4) = 2 + 4 = 6$ and $5 + (-8) = 5 - 8 = -3$.

Integers appear in everyday life: temperatures below $0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, heights below sea level or a bank account with **debt**. If a debt is cancelled, the balance goes up: $-15 - (-10) = -5$.

### Task 2: Temperature difference

In the morning the thermometer shows $-6\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$, at noon $4\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$. By how many degrees has it got warmer?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Difference: $4 - (-6) = 4 + 6 = 10$. It has got $10\,^\circ\mathrm{C}$ warmer: $-6 + 10 = 4$. [Show on the thermometer](?scene=thermo&a=-6&b=10)

</details>

### Task 3: The penguin dives

A penguin stands on an iceberg $7\,\text{m}$ above sea level and then dives $15\,\text{m}$ down. Where is it now?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$7 - 15 = -8$, so $8\,\text{m}$ below sea level. [Show in the simulation](?scene=meer&a=7&op=minus&b=15)

</details>

### Task 4: Bank balance

Tim's account is at $-12$ €. First a bill of $9$ € is debited, then he pays in $30$ €. What is the balance now?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$-12 - 9 = -21$ and $-21 + 30 = 9$. Tim now has a credit of $9$ €. [Show the first step](?scene=konto&a=-12&op=minus&b=9)

</details>

### Task 5: Explain

Use the walking model to explain why $5 - (-3)$ gives the same result as $5 + 3$.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

For $5 - (-3)$ the frog turns around because of the minus sign and faces left. Because $-3$ is negative, it hops $3$ steps backwards – that is, to the right. For $5 + 3$ it faces right and hops $3$ steps forwards, also to the right. Both times it lands on $8$. [Compare](?a=5&op=minus&b=-3)

</details>

### Task 6: Absolute value

Which integers have the absolute value $7$? Which integers have an absolute value less than $3$?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$7$ and $-7$ have the absolute value $7$. The integers $-2, -1, 0, 1$ and $2$ have an absolute value less than $3$.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** About grades 5/6 (negative numbers, ordering, opposite and absolute value on the number line; adding and subtracting integers, strategies with credit and debt).
- **Models:** The simulation links the arrow model with the walking model. For subtraction the arrow of the second number is drawn dashed and flips to the opposite direction when the frog turns around – this makes the rule “subtracting means adding the opposite” visible. Operation signs and signs of numbers have different colours and roles.
- **Controls:** Drag the blue start point and the arrow tip; press **Go!** to show the steps. If “Show the result right away” is switched off (`show=0`), the result and the arrow only appear after **Go!**; the visible part of the number line then does not give away the direction.
- **Real-life contexts:** Thermometer, sea level and bank balance each describe the actual change. The bank account is especially suitable for subtracting negative numbers (“a debt is cancelled”).
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - “Subtracting always makes things smaller” – $2 - (-4) = 6$ is greater than $2$.
  - Rules of multiplication are carried over: $-3 + (-4) = +7$ instead of $-7$.
  - When ordering, the absolute values are compared: “$-5 > -2$ because $5 > 2$.”
  - Signs of numbers and operation signs are mixed up; brackets around negative numbers help: $3 - (-2)$.
- All numbers lie between $-20$ and $20$, the results between $-40$ and $40$.
