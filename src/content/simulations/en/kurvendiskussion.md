## What is it about?

1. Find $f'$ and $f''$. 2. Symmetry (only even exponents: symmetric about the $y$-axis; only odd: about the origin) and behaviour for $x \to \pm\infty$ (degree and sign of the leading coefficient). 3. Zeros. 4. Necessary condition: $f'(x) = 0$ gives candidates. 5. Sufficient: a sign change of $f'$ from $+$ to $-$ gives a maximum, from $-$ to $+$ a minimum; no sign change gives a terrace point (or the second-derivative test). 6. Inflection points: $f''(x) = 0$ with a sign change. 7. Monotonicity and curvature.

Below the graph of $f$ the graphs of $f'$ and $f''$ are drawn with their sign coloured, so the connections can be read vertically.

*Example:* $x^3 - 3x$ has $H(-1 \mid 2)$, $T(1 \mid -2)$ and $W(0 \mid 0)$. Careful: $x^4$ has a minimum at $0$ although $f''(0) = 0$.

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- Switch values off and let students compute each step first.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** treating $f'(x) = 0$ as sufficient; concluding “no extremum” from $f''(x_0) = 0$.
