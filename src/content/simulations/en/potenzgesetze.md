## What is it about?

A **power** $a^n$ is shorthand for a product of equal factors. The laws follow by counting factors: $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$ (e.g. $2^3 \cdot 2^4 = 2^7 = 128$), $a^m : a^n = a^{m-n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$, $(ab)^n = a^n b^n$.

Going down the ladder $2^3, 2^2, 2^1$ means dividing by 2 each time, which leads to $2^0 = 1$ and $2^{-n} = 1/2^n$. Fractional exponents are roots: $a^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{a^m}$, e.g. $8^{2/3} = 4$, $4^{-1/2} = 0.5$, $\sqrt2 \cdot \sqrt8 = 4$.

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Typical mistakes:** $a^m \cdot a^n = a^{mn}$; $a^0 = 0$; $(a + b)^n = a^n + b^n$.
