## What is it about?

Two rays from a common point $Z$ are cut by two **parallel** lines, at $A$, $A'$ on one ray and $B$, $B'$ on the other. Then

$$
\frac{ZA'}{ZA} = \frac{ZB'}{ZB} \quad \text{(1st theorem)} \qquad \frac{A'B'}{AB} = \frac{ZA'}{ZA} \quad \text{(2nd theorem)}
$$

The common value is the **scale factor** $k$; triangle $ZA'B'$ is an enlargement or reduction of $ZAB$. In the **X shape** the parallels lie on different sides of $Z$ – the theorems still hold, always measuring from $Z$. Without parallels the ratios differ; conversely, equal ratios in the 1st theorem mean the lines are parallel.

## Try it

1. Drag $A'$, $A$ and $B$ and watch the fractions. Try [$k = 0.5$](?k=0.5&a=6&b=7).
2. Compare with the [X shape](?mode=x&a=4&b=5&k=0.75&th=40).
3. [Not parallel](?par=0&kb=1.4): move $B'$ until the ratios match.
4. [Tree height](?mode=baum) and [pond width](?mode=teich): drag the sun or $A'$.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Missing length

$ZA = 3\,\text{cm}$, $ZA' = 7.5\,\text{cm}$, $ZB = 4\,\text{cm}$, $AB \parallel A'B'$. Find $ZB'$. [Load task](?a=3&b=4&k=2.5&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$ZB' = 4\,\text{cm} \cdot 2.5 = 10\,\text{cm}$. [Show](?a=3&b=4&k=2.5)

</details>

### Task 2: The tree

A $1.5\,\text{m}$ stick stands $2\,\text{m}$ from the shadow tip, the tree $16\,\text{m}$. How tall is the tree? [Load task](?mode=baum&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$h = 1.5\,\text{m} \cdot \frac{16}{2} = 12\,\text{m}$. [Show](?mode=baum)

</details>

### Task 3: The pond

With $ZA' = \frac{1}{4} ZA$ and $ZB' = \frac{1}{4} ZB$ one measures $A'B' = 6\,\text{m}$. How wide is the pond? [Load task](?mode=teich&show=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$AB = 4 \cdot 6\,\text{m} = 24\,\text{m}$. [Show](?mode=teich)

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- Let students conjecture “the ratios stay the same – but only if …”, then test the non-parallel case.
- The tree and pond tasks can be recreated outdoors with a stick, tape measure and ranging poles.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** using $AA'$ instead of $ZA'$ in the 2nd theorem, applying the theorems without parallels, adding lengths across $Z$ in the X shape.
