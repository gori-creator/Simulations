## What is it about?

A charge moving perpendicular to a magnetic field feels the **Lorentz force** $F = q \cdot v \cdot B$, perpendicular to $v$ and $B$. For electrons the **left-hand rule** gives the direction (thumb: motion, index finger: field, middle finger: force). The force only changes the direction, so the electrons move on a circle.

- Acceleration: $v = \sqrt{2 \cdot (e/m) \cdot U}$.
- Helmholtz coils: $B = (4/5)^{3/2} \cdot \mu_0 \cdot n \cdot I / R$ with $n = 130$, $R = 15\,\text{cm}$.
- Circular path: $e v B = m v^2 / r$, so $r = \frac{1}{B}\sqrt{2 U m / e}$ and $e/m = \frac{2U}{B^2 r^2}$.

The marks on the ladder give the **diameter** (2 to 10 cm). Set the beam to a mark, record data points and plot $2U$ against $B^2 r^2$: the slope is $e/m \approx 1.76 \cdot 10^{11}\,\text{C/kg}$.

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Common stumbling blocks:** “The Lorentz force speeds the electrons up.” – It only changes the direction. Diameter vs. radius: the marks give the diameter.
