## What is it about?

**Two intersecting lines** form four angles $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\delta$ at the point $S$.

- **Vertical angles** lie opposite each other and are **equal**: $\alpha = \gamma$, $\beta = \delta$.
- **Adjacent angles** lie side by side on a line and **add up to $180^\circ$**: $\alpha + \beta = 180^\circ$.

For $\alpha = 65^\circ$: $\gamma = 65^\circ$ and $\beta = \delta = 115^\circ$; all four together make $360^\circ$.

**Transversal:** the lines $g$ and $h$ are crossed by a third line $s$, giving the angles $\alpha_1, \dots, \delta_1$ at $g$ and $\alpha_2, \dots, \delta_2$ at $h$.

| Pair | Shape | if $g \parallel h$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| corresponding angles | F | equal |
| alternate angles | Z | equal |
| co-interior angles | C (in German books: E) | add up to $180^\circ$ |

Sliding the crossing at $g$ along $s$ onto the crossing at $h$ makes $g$ cover $h$ exactly when $g \parallel h$. Conversely, equal corresponding angles mean the lines are parallel.

## Try it

1. Drag the handles on $g$ and $h$. Which angles always stay equal? [Load example](?w=65&a=20)
2. Press “Join adjacent angles” and “Turn vertical angle”.
3. Switch to the [transversal](?mode=doppel) and press “Slide along s”.
4. Try [alternate angles](?mode=doppel&rel=wechsel&g=62&s=60) with “Turn by 180°”, then [non-parallel lines](?mode=doppel&par=0&g=50&h=58).
5. Give reasons for every angle in the [exercise mode](?mode=aufgabe).

## Tasks

### Task 1: Four angles

Two lines intersect at $\alpha = 38^\circ$. Find $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\delta$. [Load task](?w=38&a=10&vals=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\gamma = 38^\circ$ (vertical angle), $\beta = \delta = 180^\circ - 38^\circ = 142^\circ$ (adjacent angles). [Show](?w=38&a=10)

</details>

### Task 2: Eight angles at parallels

$g \parallel h$ and $\alpha_1 = 62^\circ$. Find all eight angles. [Load task](?mode=doppel&g=62&vals=0&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

All angles are $62^\circ$ ($\alpha_1, \gamma_1, \alpha_2, \gamma_2$) or $180^\circ - 62^\circ = 118^\circ$ ($\beta_1, \delta_1, \beta_2, \delta_2$). [Show](?mode=doppel&g=62)

</details>

### Task 3: Parallel or not?

$s$ crosses $g$ at $\alpha_1 = 50^\circ$ and $h$ at $\alpha_2 = 58^\circ$. Are $g$ and $h$ parallel? [Load task](?mode=doppel&par=0&g=50&h=58&vals=0)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

No: the corresponding angles $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_2$ differ by $8^\circ$.

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- **Use:** grade 7, angles in figures, as groundwork for the angle sum of a triangle. The exercise mode shows a short chain of reasons for every tapped angle – students could give their own reasons first and compare.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - “Corresponding angles are always equal” – only for parallel lines.
  - Mixing up vertical and adjacent angles.
  - Forgetting to state “because $g \parallel h$” in a reason.
