## What is it about?

Every quantity of straight-line motion has a counterpart in rotation:

| linear | rotation |
| --- | --- |
| distance $s$ | angle $\varphi$ |
| velocity $v$ | angular velocity $\omega$ |
| mass $m$ | **moment of inertia** $J$ |
| momentum $p = m v$ | **angular momentum** $L = J \omega$ |
| kinetic energy $\frac{1}{2} m v^2$ | rotational energy $\frac{1}{2} J \omega^2$ |

### Moment of inertia

How hard a body is to set spinning depends not only on its mass but on **how far the mass is from the axis**. For a point mass at distance $r$: $J = m r^2$ – twice the distance, four times the moment of inertia.

### Conservation of angular momentum

Without external torque, $L = J_1 \omega_1 = J_2 \omega_2$. When a skater pulls in her arms, $J$ decreases, so $\omega$ must increase. The rotational energy increases: she does work pulling the arms in.

### Rolling race

Bodies of equal mass and radius roll down a slope; the one with its mass **close to the axis** wins, because less energy goes into rotation: $a = \frac{g \sin\alpha}{1 + J/(m r^2)}$. Solid sphere $\frac{2}{5}$, disc $\frac{1}{2}$, ring $1$; a frictionless sliding box does not rotate at all.

## Exercises

**1. Pirouette.** One turn per second with arms out (0.8 m); $J$ drops from 5.68 to 0.88 kg m². New rate? [Load exercise](?mode=pirouette&r=0.2&w0=1&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$\omega_2 = 1 \cdot \frac{5.68}{0.88} \approx 6.4$ turns per second.

</details>

**2. Where does the energy come from?** Compare rotational energy before and after. [Load exercise](?mode=pirouette&r=0.2&w0=1&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

About 112 J before and 722 J after – the work done pulling in the arms.

</details>

**3. Rolling race.** Ball, disc and ring roll down a 2 m ramp at 15°. Order and time for the ball? [Load exercise](?mode=rollen&alpha=15&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Ball, disc, ring. Ball: $a \approx 1.81\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$, $t \approx 1.49\,\text{s}$ (ring 1.78 s).

</details>

**4. Does mass matter?** Does a heavy ball beat a light ball of the same size?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

No – only the ratio $J/(m r^2)$ appears, which is $\frac{2}{5}$ for every solid sphere.

</details>
