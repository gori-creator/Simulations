## What is it about?

Electric and magnetic fields exert forces on charged particles. That lets us accelerate them, deflect them and even measure their charge.

### Acceleration

In an **electron gun** electrons are accelerated from a hot cathode to the anode: $e U_B = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2 e U_B / m}$. 1000 V already gives about 19,000 km per second.

### Deflection

Between charged plates a constant transverse force acts and the path is a **parabola**: $y = \frac{U_y}{4 d U_B} x^2$ – like a horizontal throw. Afterwards the electron flies straight to the screen (cathode-ray tube).

### Millikan experiment

An oil droplet hovers when $q \cdot \frac{U}{d} = m g$. Millikan found that charge always comes in **integer multiples** of the **elementary charge** $e = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.

### Cyclotron

In a magnetic field charges move on circles, $r = \frac{m v}{q B}$. In a **cyclotron**, protons gain energy at every gap crossing between the two "dees"; the orbital frequency $f = \frac{q B}{2\pi m}$ does not depend on speed.

### Hall effect

Current through a plate in a magnetic field: carriers are pushed sideways and build up the **Hall voltage** $U_H = \frac{B I}{n e d}$ – tiny in metals, large in semiconductors (Hall probes).

### Mass spectrometer

A mass spectrometer sorts atoms by their mass. First a **velocity filter** (crossed electric and magnetic fields) only lets ions with $v = \frac{E}{B}$ through. Then they fly on a semicircle with $r = \frac{m \cdot v}{q \cdot B}$ in a magnetic field – heavier ions on a larger one. This separates isotopes such as chlorine-35 and chlorine-37, dates archaeological finds or detects doping. For uranium the masses of U-235 and U-238 differ by just over 1 % – which is why enrichment is so difficult.

### Helical path and aurora

If a charged particle flies at an angle to the magnetic field, the Lorentz force only acts on the part of the velocity across the field. The particle circles and at the same time keeps moving along the field lines: a **helix**. This is how the Earth’s magnetic field guides electrons and protons from the Sun to the poles. There they collide with air molecules and make them glow – the **aurora**.

## Exercises

**1. Electron speed** after 1000 V? [Load exercise](?mode=laengs&Ua=1000&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

About $1.9 \cdot 10^7\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$, 6 % of the speed of light.

</details>

**2. Deflection.** Plates 5 cm long, 2 cm apart, $U_B = 1000\,\text{V}$, $U_y = 100\,\text{V}$. Deflection at the end of the plates? [Load exercise](?mode=quer&Ua=1000&Ud=100&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$y \approx 3.1\,\text{mm}$, independent of mass and charge.

</details>

**3. Millikan.** $m = 1.88 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\text{kg}$ hovers at 192 V, plates 5 mm apart. Number of elementary charges? [Load exercise](?mode=millikan&n=3&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$q \approx 4.8 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{C} = 3e$.

</details>

**4. Hall probe.** Why is the Hall voltage much smaller in copper? [Load exercise](?mode=hall&stoff=cu&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

Copper has about $10^{29}$ free electrons per m³, a semiconductor about $10^{22}$; fewer carriers move faster and feel a larger force.

</details>

**Separating isotopes.** Singly charged chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 ions enter a magnetic field with $B = 0.5\,\text{T}$ at $v = 10^5\,\text{m/s}$. How far apart are their impact points? [Load task](?mode=spektro&probe=chlor&B2=0.5&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

$r = \frac{m v}{e B}$ with $m = A \cdot 1.66 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$: $r_{35} \approx 7.26\,\text{cm}$, $r_{37} \approx 7.67\,\text{cm}$. The impact points are at $2r$, so about $0.8\,\text{cm}$ apart.

</details>
