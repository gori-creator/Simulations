## What is it about?

At the point where light hits a mirror we draw the **normal**, the line perpendicular to the mirror. The angle of incidence $\alpha$ and the angle of reflection $\alpha'$ are both measured from the normal, and

$$
\alpha' = \alpha .
$$

Turning the mirror by $\varphi$ turns the reflected ray by $2\varphi$.

The **image** in a plane mirror is virtual, upright, the same size and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it ($b = g$); front and back are swapped. Two **angled mirrors** also reflect each other’s images; if $360^\circ : \alpha$ is an even number there are $\frac{360^\circ}{\alpha} - 1$ images.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Angle to the surface

A ray makes an angle of $20^\circ$ with the mirror surface. What are the angles of incidence and reflection? [Load task](?la=70&gl=1&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Angles are measured from the normal: $\alpha = \alpha' = 90^\circ - 20^\circ = 70^\circ$.

</details>

### Task 2: Turning the mirror

The laser stays fixed and the mirror is turned by $10^\circ$. By how much does the reflected ray turn? [Load task](?la=30&tilt=10&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

By $2 \cdot 10^\circ = 20^\circ$.

</details>

### Task 3: Angled mirrors

How many images do you see with mirrors at $45^\circ$? [Load task](?mode=multi&w=45&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\frac{360^\circ}{45^\circ} - 1 = 7$ images.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- The angle scale on the optical disc turns with the mirror, so angles are always read from the normal.
- **Misconceptions:** “The angle of incidence is measured from the mirror surface.” “The image lies on the surface of the mirror.” “Stepping back lets you see more of yourself.” – A full-length mirror only needs to be half your height.
