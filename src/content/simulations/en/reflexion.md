## What is it about?

When light hits a mirror, it is **reflected**. At the point of incidence we draw the **normal**, the line perpendicular to the mirror. Both angles are measured **from the normal**, not from the mirror surface: the **angle of incidence** $\alpha$ and the **angle of reflection** $\alpha'$.

**Law of reflection:** $\alpha' = \alpha$. Incident ray, normal and reflected ray lie in one plane, and the light path is reversible. Turning the mirror by $\varphi$ turns the reflected ray by $2\varphi$.

**Mirror image.** Light from a point of the candle that reaches the eye after the reflection seems to come from a point **behind** the mirror. The image distance equals the object distance, $b = g$. The image is **virtual** (it cannot be caught on a screen), **upright** and the **same size**; strictly speaking, front and back are swapped. A full-length mirror only needs to be **half as tall** as the person – at any distance.

**Angled mirrors.** Two mirrors at an angle $\varphi$ produce images of images. If $360^\circ : \varphi$ is an even number, there are $n = 360^\circ/\varphi - 1$ images. Images after an odd number of reflections are reversed, after an even number they are not.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Grazing light

A ray makes an angle of $25^\circ$ with the mirror surface. Find the angles of incidence and reflection.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$\alpha = 90^\circ - 25^\circ = 65^\circ = \alpha'$. The two rays enclose $130^\circ$. [Show in the simulation](?la=65&gl=1)

</details>

### Task 2: Walking towards the mirror

Lena stands $1.5\,\text{m}$ in front of a mirror and walks towards it at $1\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$. How far away is her image, and how fast does she approach it?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The image is $1.5\,\text{m}$ behind the mirror, $3\,\text{m}$ from Lena. Both move $1\,\text{m}$ closer to the mirror per second, so they approach each other at $2\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$.

</details>

### Task 3: Full-length mirror

Tom is $1.70\,\text{m}$ tall, his eyes are at $1.60\,\text{m}$. How tall must the mirror be, and where is its lower edge?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Lower edge at half the eye height, $0.80\,\text{m}$; upper edge halfway between eyes and the top of the head, $1.65\,\text{m}$. The mirror needs to be $0.85\,\text{m}$ tall – half his height. [Show the model](?mode=image&g=30&h=30&ex=30&ey=40&mb=0&mt=50)

</details>

### Task 4: Kaleidoscope

At which angle must two mirrors stand so that you see 5 images?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$360^\circ : \varphi - 1 = 5$ gives $\varphi = 60^\circ$. [Show 60°](?mode=multi&w=60&f=0.5&r=4.5)

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- **Use:** introductory optics (law of reflection, plane mirror images), lower secondary level.
- **Optical disc:** the disc turns with the mirror, so the scale always shows the angle to the normal; the measured values are ideal.
- **Angled mirrors:** top view; the number at each image counts the reflections. The mirrors have a finite length, so not every image is visible from the eye.
- **Misconceptions:** angles measured from the mirror surface; the image lies on the mirror surface; stepping back shows more of yourself.
