## What is it about?

When light hits a mirror, it is **reflected** (thrown back). At the point of incidence we draw the **normal**, the line perpendicular to the mirror. Both angles are measured **from the normal**, not from the mirror surface: the **angle of incidence** $\alpha$ and the **angle of reflection** $\alpha'$.

**Law of reflection:** $\alpha' = \alpha$. Incident ray, normal and reflected ray lie in one plane, and the light path is reversible. Turning the mirror by $\varphi$ turns the normal with it, so the reflected ray turns by $2\varphi$.

**Mirror image.** Light from a point of the candle that reaches the eye after the reflection seems to come from a point **behind** the mirror. The image distance equals the object distance, $b = g$. The image is **virtual** (it cannot be caught on a screen), **upright** and the **same size**. People often say the image is "reversed left to right"; strictly speaking, front and back are swapped. A full-length mirror only needs to be **half as tall** as the person – at any distance.

**Angled mirrors.** Two mirrors at an angle $\varphi$ produce images of images. If $360^\circ : \varphi$ is an even number, there are $n = 360^\circ/\varphi - 1$ images, e.g. 3 images at $90^\circ$ and 5 at $60^\circ$. Otherwise the number depends on where the figure stands. Images after an odd number of reflections are reversed, after an even number they are not – two reflections cancel out. This is how a **kaleidoscope** works.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Grazing light

A ray makes an angle of $25^\circ$ with the mirror surface. Find the angles of incidence and reflection. What angle do the incident and the reflected ray enclose?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The angles are measured from the normal: $\alpha = 90^\circ - 25^\circ = 65^\circ = \alpha'$. The two rays enclose $\alpha + \alpha' = 130^\circ$. [Show in the simulation](?la=65&gl=1)

</details>

### Task 2: Turning the mirror

A laser hits a mirror at $\alpha = 30^\circ$. Now the mirror is turned by $10^\circ$ so that the angle of incidence gets smaller. What is the new angle of incidence? By how much does the reflected ray turn? [Load task](?la=30&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The normal turns with the mirror by $10^\circ$ towards the laser, so $\alpha = 30^\circ - 10^\circ = 20^\circ = \alpha'$. Before, the reflected ray was $30^\circ$ to the right of the table's vertical; now it is $20^\circ$ to the right of the turned normal, i.e. only $10^\circ$ to the right of the vertical. It has turned by $20^\circ$ – twice as much as the mirror. [Show in the simulation](?la=30&tilt=10)

</details>

### Task 3: Walking towards the mirror

Lena stands $1.5\,\text{m}$ in front of a large wall mirror. How far away is her image? She walks towards the mirror at $1\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$. How fast does she approach her image?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The image is $b = g = 1.5\,\text{m}$ behind the mirror, so $3\,\text{m}$ from Lena. If Lena moves $1\,\text{m}$ closer to the mirror, her image also moves $1\,\text{m}$ closer: the distance shrinks by $2\,\text{m}$ per second, they approach each other at $2\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$. [Move the candle](?mode=image&g=15)

</details>

### Task 4: Full-length mirror

Tom is $1.70\,\text{m}$ tall, his eyes are $1.60\,\text{m}$ above the floor. How tall must a mirror be at least so that Tom can see all of himself? At what height must its lower edge be? Hint: draw the lines of sight from his feet and from the top of his head.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Light from the feet hits the mirror at half the eye height, because the angles of incidence and reflection are equal: lower edge at $0.80\,\text{m}$. Light from the top of the head hits it halfway between eyes and head, at $1.65\,\text{m}$. So the mirror only needs to be $0.85\,\text{m}$ tall – half his height, at any distance. In the simulation the eye is directly above the candle ($40\,\text{cm}$ high, candle $30\,\text{cm}$): the mirror is needed from $20\,\text{cm}$ to $35\,\text{cm}$. </details>

### Task 5: Images in angled mirrors

a) How many images appear at $45^\circ$? b) At which angle must two mirrors stand so that you see 5 images? c) In a kaleidoscope you see a pattern with six equal parts. What is the angle between the mirrors?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

a) $360^\circ : 45^\circ - 1 = 7$ images. b) $360^\circ : \varphi = 6$, so $\varphi = 60^\circ$. c) The figure and 5 images make 6 parts: again $60^\circ$. [Show 45°](?mode=multi&w=45) · [Show 60°](?mode=multi&w=60&f=0.5&r=4.5)

</details>

### Task 6: Seeing yourself the right way round

Looking into two mirrors at $90^\circ$, you see yourself in the middle image the way others see you: if you raise your right hand, the image – like a real person facing you – raises **its** right hand. In an ordinary mirror the image seems to raise its left hand. Explain why.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The middle image is made by **two** reflections (first at one mirror, then at the other). Each reflection swaps once – two swaps cancel out. The image is only turned by $180^\circ$. Tap the image marked "2" in the simulation. [Show 90°](?mode=multi)

</details>

## Ideas for the classroom

- **Use:** introductory optics (law of reflection, plane mirror images), lower secondary level. Part 1 can replace or supplement the student experiment with the optical disc, part 2 is for practising image construction, part 3 goes further.
- **Optical disc:** the disc turns with the mirror, so the scale always shows the angle to the normal. The measured values are ideal ($\alpha' = \alpha$); with a real experiment it is worth comparing measurement errors. If the laser points at the back, nothing is reflected.
- **Mirror image:** candle, eye and mirror can be dragged. "Construction" shows the perpendicular and distances, and at the points of incidence the normal and equal angles. The field of view is constructed with the image of the eye: everything in this area can be seen in the mirror.
- **Angled mirrors:** top view; the number at each image counts the reflections. The mirrors have a finite length, so not every image is visible from the eye. The graph shows the number of images for all angles at the current position of the figure.
- **Common stumbling blocks:**
  - "The angle of incidence is measured from the mirror surface." – Both angles are measured from the normal.
  - "The image lies on the mirror surface." – It lies as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
  - "Stepping back shows more of yourself." – The part of the mirror needed stays half your height.
  - "A mirror swaps left and right." – It swaps front and back.
