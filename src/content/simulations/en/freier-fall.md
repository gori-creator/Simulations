## What is it about?

Without air resistance, free fall is a uniformly accelerated motion with the gravitational acceleration $g$ ($9.81\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$ on Earth):

$$
s = \tfrac{1}{2}\, g\, t^2 \qquad v = g \cdot t \qquad t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}.
$$

In a stroboscopic picture the gaps grow like $1 : 3 : 5 : 7$. Without air a heavy ball and a light feather fall equally fast; in air, air resistance limits the feather to a small terminal velocity. Catching a falling ruler after the distance $s$ gives your reaction time $t = \sqrt{2s/g}$.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Drop tower

A ball falls from $20\,\text{m}$. Find the fall time and the impact speed. [Load task](?_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$t = \sqrt{2 \cdot 20 / 9.81}\,\text{s} \approx 2.02\,\text{s}$, $v = g t \approx 19.8\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$.

</details>

### Task 2: Reaction time

The ruler is caught after $18\,\text{cm}$. Find the reaction time.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$t = \sqrt{2 \cdot 0.18 / 9.81}\,\text{s} \approx 0.19\,\text{s}$.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- The drop tower ignores air resistance. In the tube, air resistance is modelled proportional to $v^2$ with approximate terminal velocities ($75\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$ for the steel ball, $0.6\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}$ for the feather).
- Screen and input devices add some latency, so on-screen reaction times are usually a little longer than with a real ruler.
- **Misconception:** “Heavy objects fall faster.”
