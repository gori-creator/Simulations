## What is it about?

A bulb only lights up in a **closed circuit**: there must be a continuous path of **conductors** from one terminal of the battery through the bulb to the other terminal. A **switch** opens or closes this path.

The metal wires contain free **electrons**. They are present everywhere, even when no current flows. When the circuit is closed, they all start moving at once – outside the battery from the **negative to the positive terminal**. They are not used up in the bulb. The **conventional current** direction was defined before electrons were known: from **positive to negative**.

Circuit diagrams use standard **symbols**: battery (long thin line = positive, short thick line = negative), bulb (circle with a cross), motor (circle with M), switch, fuse. Metals and graphite are **conductors**; wood, plastic, rubber and glass are **insulators**.

A **short circuit** connects the terminals with almost no resistance: the bulb goes out and the current rises from about $0.3\,\text{A}$ to about $6\,\text{A}$ – wires and battery get hot. A **fuse** melts and breaks the circuit.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Conductor or insulator?

Predict which objects conduct, then test them. [Load task](?mode=test&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

Paper clip, coin, aluminium foil and pencil lead (graphite) conduct; the wooden stick, eraser, plastic ruler and glass rod do not. Graphite conducts less well – the bulb is dimmer.

</details>

### Task 2: Short circuit

Why does the bulb go out when a wire connects its terminals, and why is this dangerous? [Load task](?on=1&sc=1&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

The wire has almost no resistance, so the current bypasses the bulb. With nearly nothing limiting the current, it becomes about 20 times as large; the wire and the battery get hot.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- Set-up and diagram are shown side by side; hovering highlights a component in both. `view=plan`, `view=real` and `flow=none` (effects only) are useful variations.
- Model: $4.5\,\text{V}$ battery with $0.7\,\Omega$ internal resistance, $4.5\,\text{V}/0.3\,\text{A}$ bulb, motor and bell as resistors, $1\,\text{A}$ fuse. The speed of the dots only indicates the current; real electrons drift far more slowly.
- **Misconceptions:** current is “used up” in the bulb; electrons come out of the battery; one wire is enough.
