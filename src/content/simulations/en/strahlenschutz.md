## What is it about?

Radioactive substances emit **ionising radiation**, energetic enough to knock electrons out of atoms and damage cells and DNA.

| Radiation | What is it? | Range in air | Shielding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alpha (α) | helium nuclei | a few centimetres | a sheet of paper, skin |
| Beta (β) | fast electrons | a few metres | a few millimetres of aluminium |
| Gamma (γ) | high-energy photons | very far | lead, concrete – never completely |

A **Geiger counter** clicks for every particle; it also counts a little **background** without a source.

Gamma radiation decreases **exponentially** in an absorber; the **half-value thickness** of lead is about 6.5 mm: $N(d) = N_0 \cdot (1/2)^{d/d_{1/2}}$. For a point source the count rate follows the **inverse-square law** $N \sim 1/r^2$.

Protection: keep **distance**, **shield**, keep **time** short, avoid **intake**. Alpha emitters inside the body are about 20 times as harmful as gamma of the same energy.

## Exercises

**1. Which radiation?** Paper hardly changes the count rate; 5 mm aluminium reduces it almost to background. [Load exercise](?ray=beta&abs=aluminium&d=5&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

**Beta** radiation.

</details>

**2. Lead.** 25 counts/s behind 13 mm lead, 100 without. Half-value thickness? Thickness for 1 %? [Load exercise](?ray=gamma&abs=lead&d=13&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

6.5 mm; about 6.6 half-value thicknesses, roughly 43 mm.

</details>

**3. Distance.** 200 counts/s at 10 cm. At 50 cm? [Load exercise](?ray=gamma&abs=none&r=50&_hide=1)

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

1/25 of it, about 8 counts/s.

</details>

**4. Radon.** Why is the alpha emitter radon dangerous although skin stops alpha radiation?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>

It is inhaled and decays in the lungs, where no protective skin layer exists.

</details>
