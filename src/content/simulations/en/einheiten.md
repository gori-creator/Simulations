## What is it about?

A **quantity** has a **number** and a **unit**: in $3.5\,\text{km}$ the number is $3.5$ and the unit is km. Converting keeps the quantity the same; only number and unit change: $3.5\,\text{km} = 3{,}500\,\text{m}$.

The **conversion factor** between neighbouring units is $10$ for length (but $1{,}000$ between km and m), $1{,}000$ for mass and volume and $100$ for area. Also $1\,\text{a} = 100\,\text{m}^2$, $1\,\text{ha} = 10{,}000\,\text{m}^2$, $1\,\text{dm}^3 = 1\,\text{l}$ and $1\,\text{cm}^3 = 1\,\text{ml}$.

In the **unit chart** each column is one place. The ones digit goes into the column of the unit, with the decimal point to its right. To convert, move the point next to the ones column of the new unit: to a **smaller** unit it moves **right** (the number grows), to a **larger** unit it moves **left**. Empty columns are filled with **zeros**.

**Time is different:** $1\,\text{d} = 24\,\text{h}$, $1\,\text{h} = 60\,\text{min}$, $1\,\text{min} = 60\,\text{s}$. There is no chart; you multiply or divide step by step. Mixed units are common: $135\,\text{min} = 2\,\text{h}\;15\,\text{min} = 2.25\,\text{h}$.

## Ideas for the classroom

*Just suggestions – you know your class best.*

- Predict together on the projector: “Where does the point go?” Then tap **Convert**. **New task** and the switch “Show the result right away” support independent practice; **Way back ⇄** shows that converting back gives the starting value.
- **Common stumbling blocks:** using $10$ instead of $100$ or $1{,}000$ for area and volume; moving only one place between km and m; forgetting the $0$ before the point; reading $2.5\,\text{h}$ as $2\,\text{h}\;50\,\text{min}$.
