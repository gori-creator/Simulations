## What is it about?

Each linear equation in $x$ and $y$ describes a **line**. A pair $(x, y)$ that satisfies **both** equations lies on both lines – it is their **point of intersection**. Graphical method: rearrange both equations to $y = mx + c$, draw both lines, read off the intersection and check it in both equations.

- Lines intersect (different slopes): exactly one solution.
- Parallel, distinct lines (same slope): no solution.
- Identical lines: infinitely many solutions.

If the intersection does not lie on grid points, it can only be read off approximately – then an algebraic method helps.

### Task 2

Tariff A costs €10 per month plus 5 ct per minute, tariff B €4 plus 11 ct per minute. From how many minutes on is A cheaper? [Load task](?form=tarif&_hide=1)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

$0.05x + 10 = 0.11x + 4 \Rightarrow x = 100$: above 100 minutes tariff A is cheaper; at 100 minutes both cost €15.

</details>

## Notes for teachers

- Equations in slope-intercept form, in general form $ax + by = c$ (including vertical lines) or as a tariff comparison. The simulation calculates exactly with fractions and shows when a graphical reading is only approximate.
- **Misconceptions:** checking the intersection in one equation only; drawing $ax + by = c$ without rearranging.
